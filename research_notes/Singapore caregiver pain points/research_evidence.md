## 1. How many family caregivers are there, who are they, and how fast is demand growing?

### Takeaway
No single official headcount of family caregivers exists. Survey snapshots say about 1 in 7 adults aged 48–79 are caregivers (SMU ROSA, Nov 2024, n=7,692) and 7.4% of adults aged 67+ (Duke-NUS CARE, 2023–24, up from 6% in 2019). Many caregivers are older themselves, many work full-time (45%), and demand will rise sharply by 2030: one in four Singaporeans will be 65+, about 100,000 seniors will need ADL help, and the old-age support ratio will fall to 2.7.

### Cited Findings
- **SMU ROSA, Singapore Life Panel, November 2024, n=7,692** (residents aged 48–79 and their spouses): about 1 in 7 (nearly 14%) are caregivers **[V]** — [SMU news, 7 Apr 2025](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/07/1-7-older-adults-juggle-work-chronic-illness-and-unpaid-caregiving); [SMU news, 9 Apr 2025](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/09/smu-report-nearly-14-older-adults-are-caregivers-over-half-aged-60-and-above)
  - 45.24% still work full-time; 57.25% are women; 33.99% have 2+ chronic conditions themselves **[V]**
  - 23.35% care for more than one person; 54.09% of care recipients are aged 80+; 66.09% of recipients have age-related limitations; 39.45% live with the care recipient **[V]**
  - Tasks: 59.23% manage the recipient's finances; 44.63% give daily care; 44.07% **arrange external care services** **[V]**
  - Headline: "over half aged 60 and above" (the body text gives no exact figure) **[V headline only]**
  - Full brief: [ROSA, "Profiles and well-being of caregivers in Singapore" (Mar 2025)](https://rosa.smu.edu.sg/sites/rosa.smu.edu.sg/files/Briefs/Mar25/Profiles%20and%20well-being%20of%20caregivers%20in%20Singapore.pdf) (PDF could not be parsed here)
- **Duke-NUS CARE national survey:** informal caregivers made up 6% of adults aged 67+ in 2019 and 7.4% in 2023–2024 **[S]** — [Duke-NUS CARE, "Seniors caring for seniors" (SIGNS study)](https://www.duke-nus.edu.sg/care/news-events/news/the-signs-study/seniors-caring-for-seniors--what-caregiving-looks-like-in-a-super-aged-society)
- **Employed caregivers (MOM, first survey year 2025):** around 6 in 10 full-time employed residents who care for family or non-family members spent **20+ hours a week** on caregiving **[V]** — [MOM PQ reply, 6 May 2026](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0506-written-answer-to-pq-on-employed-caregivers-who-provide-substantial-informal-care)
- **Projections:** by 2030 one in four Singaporeans will be 65+; the old-age support ratio falls from 4.8 to 2.7 **[S]** — [MOH, "Preparations to train and build up pool of informal caregivers"](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/preparations-to-train-and-build-up-pool-of-informal-caregivers-given-anticipated-demand-as-population-ages/)
- About 100,000 seniors will need help with at least one ADL by 2030 **[S]** — [MOH COS speech, Tan Kiat How, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-tan-kiat-how--senior-minister-of-state--ministry-of-digital-development-and-information---ministry-of-health--at-the-ministry-of-health-committee-of-supply-debate-2026--on-5-march-2026/)
- About 122,000 seniors are projected to live alone by 2030 **[S, secondary media]** — [Mothership, Nov 2024](https://mothership.sg/2024/11/122000-seniors-live-alone-singapore)
- **Migrant domestic workers (MDWs):**
  - Duke-NUS TraCE survey (2019–2020, n=278 caregiver–care recipient pairs): MDWs spent about **42 hours a week** caring for the senior **[S]** — [Duke-NUS media release, Aug 2024](https://www.duke-nus.edu.sg/newshub/media-coverage/informal-caregiving-for-seniors-valued-at-s1.28-billion-annually); paper: Azman et al., *Aging and Health Research* 2024, [doi:10.1016/j.ahr.2024.100193](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ahr.2024.100193)
  - About 4,300 MDWs (about 1.4% of all MDWs) are registered to care for the employer or household members at a secondary address (as of Aug 2025) **[V]** — [MOM PQ reply, 22 Sep 2025](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2025/0922-written-answer-to-pq-on-data-request-on-mdws-undertaking-caregiving-responsibilities). MOM gives no share of MDWs who care for older people.
  - Earlier SingHealth/Duke-NUS study: close to half of foreign domestic caregivers lack the confidence or skills for seniors' healthcare needs **[S]** — [SingHealth Duke-NUS](https://www.singhealthdukenus.com.sg/news/research/first-local-study-shows-close-to-half-of-the-foreign-domestic-caregivers-lack-the-confidence-skills-to-support-the-healthcare-needs-of-elderly)
- **Task sharing:** Duke-NUS "Sharing in caring" (n=278 primary family caregivers, home-dwelling older adults; TraCE data): "Shared-Diverse" was the largest pattern at 39%; solo caregivers were the most vulnerable in employment capacity and mental health **[S]** — [Duke-NUS CARE](https://www.duke-nus.edu.sg/care/publications/research-publications/detail/index/sharing-in-caring-family-caregiving-task-sharing-patterns-for-older-adults-in-singapore)

### Inferences
- A navigator should assume that the person planning care is often a **working adult in their 50s or 60s with health problems of their own**, sometimes caring for 2+ people, and that the plan often runs through an MDW. The tool needs an "MDW in the household" branch, covering MDW training, the levy concession and MDW hours.
- 44% of older caregivers already "arrange external care services" and 59% manage finances. These are exactly the tasks a planning, cost and next-steps tool supports, so the addressable user base is large.

### Gaps
- No official national count of family caregivers. The ROSA and CARE figures cover age-limited panels, and their definitions differ, so they are not additive.
- No verified current figure for the share of care recipients who rely on an MDW. Older SSIC data exist but were not fetched.
- The exact ROSA figure for caregivers aged 60+ was not verified because the PDF could not be parsed.

## 2. Awareness of services, respite use, information-seeking and stress (ROSA, Duke-NUS CARE, AIC, NUS, IPS, Lien)

### Takeaway
Awareness is the main bottleneck. The AIC 2024 caregiver survey found only about 41% of caregivers aware of support services, although 66% of those who used them were satisfied. ROSA found that only half of older caregivers know about respite care, and 82.83% of those who know have never used it. Low awareness, not dissatisfaction, is the dominant problem.

### Cited Findings
- **AIC 2024 caregiver survey** (ICIC 2026 conference abstract, published in *IJIC*): about **41%** of caregivers aware of available support services; **66%** of service users satisfied; sole caregivers reported higher strain on finances and family support than those with co-caregivers; the authors recommend targeted outreach and better communication **[S; abstract page returned 403]** — [IJIC abstract ICIC26535](https://reference-global.com/article/10.5334/ijic.ICIC26535). The sample size could not be retrieved. This is a conference abstract, so the figures may be revised.
- AIC runs ongoing caregiver surveys — [AIC ongoing surveys](https://www.aic.sg/about-us/ongoing-surveys); earlier release: [AIC media release, 20 May 2023](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/agencyforinb6cc-agencyforin73f5-production08ac-d178/media/agency-for-integrated-care/Files/Media-Releases/2023/AIC-CG-Media-Release_20-May-2023_newsroom.pdf) (not fetched)
- **ROSA, Nov 2024, n=7,692:** 50.09% of caregivers aware of respite care; **82.83% of those aware have never used it** **[V]** — [SMU news, 7 Apr 2025](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/07/1-7-older-adults-juggle-work-chronic-illness-and-unpaid-caregiving). The authors suggest the cause may be low awareness, access difficulties or stigma **[S]**.
- **Duke-NUS CARE commentary** (Azman & Lee, 15 May 2025): financial assistance such as the enhanced Home Caregiving Grant is not enough; caregivers need time, respite and reliable support; social isolation and family conflict are flagged, with peer support or mediation suggested **[S]** — [Duke-NUS commentary](https://www.duke-nus.edu.sg/newshub/media-coverage/commentary--caregivers-need-more-support.-we-owe-it-to-them)
- **Stress and depression (older evidence):**
  - Singapore Survey on Informal Caregiving (2011 data): primary stressors, MDW assistance, perceived emotional support and caregiver health were associated with depressive symptoms **[S]** — [Int Psychogeriatrics](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-psychogeriatrics/article/depressive-symptoms-among-informal-caregivers-of-older-adults-insights-from-the-singapore-survey-on-informal-caregiving/BCFAF1D5BA6F3F23BA9CE6010DFE7D6D)
  - WiSE study (n=693 dyads): more care needs, BPSD and dementia drove caregiver burden **[S]** — [PMC4720142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4720142)
  - ZBI-9 cut-off ≥13 discriminates depressive symptoms (sensitivity 70%, specificity 75%) in a Singapore memory-clinic sample **[S; date unconfirmed]** — [PMC9946168](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9946168)

### Inferences
- The awareness gap is about 59% (AIC), and only about half know about respite (ROSA). This is the strongest quantitative case for a "what exists and what's my next step" tool. Satisfaction among users (66%) suggests services help once people find them.
- Respite non-use among those who know about it (83%) points to access or friction barriers, such as how to book, cost and eligibility, not just unawareness. A tool that shows eligibility, cost after subsidy and how to book could address this.
- A short screen such as ZBI-9 could be embedded to flag caregivers at risk of depression and route them to support.

### Gaps
- No verified figures found from IPS or Lien Foundation in 2020–2026 on caregiver awareness or information-seeking. These searches were not completed within the tool budget.
- AIC survey sample size and methodology are unverified.
- No recent (2023–2026) Singapore prevalence of caregiver depression or high burden was verified.

## 3. Documented information needs and decision points (hospital discharge, dementia diagnosis)

### Takeaway
Singapore evidence identifies **information** as a core unmet need alongside emotional support, finances and accessible facilities. The two key decision points are **hospital-to-home transitions** and **a new dementia diagnosis**. Most of this evidence is qualitative, with small samples.

### Cited Findings
- Perceived unmet needs of informal dementia caregivers in Singapore fall into four categories: emotional/social support, **information**, financial support, and accessible, appropriate facilities **[S]** — [Int Psychogeriatrics, "Perceived unmet needs of informal caregivers of people with dementia in Singapore"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-psychogeriatrics/article/abs/perceived-unmet-needs-of-informal-caregivers-of-people-with-dementia-in-singapore/742D38FA5A4AA9118A7B81A1D4EE444B)
- Qualitative study of 11 main family caregivers of people with **newly diagnosed** dementia (tertiary hospital, Singapore) **[S]** — [PubMed 36594495](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36594495/)
- Help-seeking around diagnosis: 29 informal dementia caregivers in Singapore interviewed in 2019–2020 (2024 publication) **[S]** — [PMC11282579](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11282579/)
- **Hospital discharge:** a 2019 cross-sectional SGH study of caregiver-valued needs versus nurse perception noted that caregivers may be unprepared for the care required after discharge **[S]** — [PMC6875807](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6875807). The "Carer Matters" pilot (hospital-to-home, caregiver-centred, feasibility trial including dementia) **[S]** — [BMC Health Serv Res 2022](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12913-022-08317-3.pdf)
- ROSA: 44.07% of older caregivers arrange external care services and 59.23% manage the recipient's finances **[V]** — [SMU news](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/07/1-7-older-adults-juggle-work-chronic-illness-and-unpaid-caregiving)
- *(Comparable, Australia, labelled)*: 29 dementia caregivers reported inconsistent medication information at discharge and called for structured discharge education **[S]** — [PMC7740337](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7740337/)

### Inferences
- The tool's two highest-value entry flows are "Mum/Dad is being discharged, now what?" and "We just got a dementia diagnosis." Both are moments when caregivers are unprepared and actively looking for information.
- Financial navigation (means-testing, grants, MediFund/ElderShield/CareShield) is a documented unmet need, so cost estimation addresses a named gap.

### Gaps
- No quantitative Singapore survey was found measuring information needs at discharge (for example, the % who felt unprepared). The evidence is mostly qualitative or small-sample.
- No data found on where caregivers search first (AIC hotline, polyclinic, Google, social workers).

## 4. Burnout, job loss or reduced work, and financial strain

### Takeaway
About 87,100 residents were outside the labour force for caregiving reasons in 2024 (85.7% women). About 12,000–13,200 a year are out specifically to care for parents, and only about 1 in 4 caregivers return to work within two years. Unpaid eldercare time is valued at S$1.28 billion a year, and at about S$1,745 a month for a senior with 3+ ADL limitations.

### Cited Findings
- **2024: about 87,100 residents outside the labour force due to caregiving (all types); 85.7% women** **[S]** — [MOM PQ reply, Sep 2025](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2025/0925-written-answer-to-pq-on-caregiving-related-unemployment)
- **Residents outside the labour force to care for parents** **[V]** — [MOM PQ reply, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0224-written-answer-to-pq-on-mandating-eldercare-leave):
  - 2021: 12,000 (72.2% women) · 2022: 12,400 · 2023: 12,000 · 2024: 13,200 · 2025: 12,300 (59.9% women; 40.1% men, up from 27.8%)
  - Ages 2025: 40.2% aged 50–59; 34.5% aged 60+; 16.1% aged 40–49; 9.2% under 40
  - On average 11.9%, about **1,500 people a year**, had left their last job within the preceding year
  - The government has not mandated eldercare leave and favours flexible work arrangements (FWAs)
- About 1 in 4 residents outside the labour force mainly for caregiving returned to work within two years **[S]** — [MOM PQ reply, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0302-written-answer-to-pq-on-time-taken-for-caregivers-to-return-to-workforce)
- About 6 in 10 full-time employed caregivers spend 20+ hrs a week caregiving (2025) **[V]** — [MOM PQ reply, 6 May 2026](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0506-written-answer-to-pq-on-employed-caregivers-who-provide-substantial-informal-care)
- Duke-NUS: unpaid informal care for seniors valued at about **S$1.28 billion a year** (TraCE, 2019–2020, n=278 dyads) **[S]** — [Duke-NUS media release](https://www.duke-nus.edu.sg/newshub/media-coverage/informal-caregiving-for-seniors-valued-at-s1.28-billion-annually); [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2024-08-caregiving-seniors-singapore-valued-s128.html)
- CARE study (2018–2022): family caregiving time for an older adult with 3+ ADL limitations worth about **S$1,745 a month** **[S]** — [Duke-NUS commentary, May 2025](https://www.duke-nus.edu.sg/newshub/media-coverage/commentary--caregivers-need-more-support.-we-owe-it-to-them)
- Duke-NUS also reports more men leaving work to care for parents **[S, headline only]** — [Duke-NUS CARE](https://www.duke-nus.edu.sg/care/news-events/news/articles/choosing-career-or-care--more-men-in-singapore-exiting-the-workforce-to-look-after-their-parents). This is consistent with the MOM male share rising from 27.8% (2021) to 40.1% (2025) **[V]**.
- AIC 2024: sole caregivers report higher financial strain **[S]** — [IJIC abstract](https://reference-global.com/article/10.5334/ijic.ICIC26535)
- Background (older): AWARE "Make Care Count" (2019) on employment and CPF impacts of eldercare — [AWARE report](https://www.aware.org.sg/wp-content/uploads/AWARE-Make-Care-Count-Report-19-9-19.pdf) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- Job exit is costly and sticky (about 1 in 4 return within 2 years). A tool that shows FWAs, day care, home care or respite *before* someone quits could have real economic value. The S$1,745/month time-value is a usable benchmark for "cost of doing it yourself" in a cost estimator.
- Caregivers who leave work are concentrated at ages 50–69, which lines up with the ROSA caregiver profile.

### Gaps
- No verified % of working caregivers who *reduced hours* (as opposed to exiting). The ROSA brief may have this, but the PDF could not be parsed.
- No verified out-of-pocket care-spend figures per household from a 2020–2026 survey.

## 5. Evaluations of navigation and care-coordination interventions

### Takeaway
Singapore's evaluated coordination programmes are mostly **hospital-to-community transitional care** (ACTION, SGH transitional care, KTPH home-based care) measured on readmissions and ED use, not on caregiver outcomes. Recent, rigorous evaluations of caregiver-facing navigation in Singapore are scarce.

### Cited Findings
- **AIC ACTION programme** (national transitional care; care coordinators in hospital plus post-discharge follow-up), n=4,132 adults aged 65+ across five public hospitals (2009–2010), compared against propensity-matched controls; aimed to reduce unplanned admissions and ED visits; caregiver QoL surveys were administered, but caregiver results were not retrieved **[S]** — [J Am Geriatr Soc 2014;62:747–753 (PDF via caretransitions.org)](https://caretransitions.org/files/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/effectiveness-of-a-national-transitional-care-program-in-reducing-acute-care-use.pdf)
- **SGH transitional care RCT**: components were post-discharge surveillance, specialist follow-up coordination, patient education with **caregiver coaching**, and activation of community and social services; 2010 baseline 30-day readmission was 11.6% overall and 19% for people aged 65+ **[S]** — [PMC4843175](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4843175)
- **CGH CH-ACTION** (conference slides, 2022): 30-day readmission about 40% in controls versus about 10% in cases. This is not peer-reviewed, so treat with caution **[S]** — [YCH CCEA 2022 slides](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/622/687ae089-e6f3-4f7f-ba72-cf29725ea536/C_447_YCH_CCEA_2022_Beyond_the_Walls_Promoting_Safe_Transitions_Home.pdf)
- **KTPH home-based transitional care**: an evaluation-methods abstract; a KTPH paper notes a "paucity of up-to-date local and regional studies" on transitional home care outcomes **[S]** — [IJIC 2845](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.5334/ijic.2845.pdf); [Penkunas et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5624063)
- NUS Social Service Research Centre / transitional care research report — [NUS CT Research Report](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/nai/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2020/07/CT-Research-Report_Final.pdf) (not fetched)
- Carer Matters (hospital-to-home caregiver programme) feasibility trial — [BMC HSR 2022](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12913-022-08317-3.pdf) **[S]**

### Inferences
- Evidence supports coordination at discharge for reducing acute care use. Caregiver-reported outcomes (burden, preparedness, awareness) are the gap. A navigator tool could measure awareness and preparedness before and after use to fill it.

### Gaps
- No Singapore RCT or quasi-experiment found evaluating a **digital** caregiver navigation or planning tool.
- Comparable OECD and Asian evidence (for example US/UK dementia care navigator trials, Japan's care-manager model) was not searched within budget. A separate pass should label it explicitly as non-Singapore.
- ICIC 2025/2026 abstracts beyond the AIC awareness study were not systematically searched.
