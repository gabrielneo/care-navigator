# Public voice: what Singapore family caregivers say about finding and arranging eldercare

Scope note (read first): the first-person evidence I could collect is thin. Reddit (r/singapore, r/askSingapore) could not be fetched ("unable to fetch from www.reddit.com"), and web search did not index Reddit threads on this topic. HardwareZone thread replies are partly hidden behind a login and age check. ST Forum, CNA, TODAY and Mothership pieces did not appear in searches. The Lien Foundation 2024 report PDF downloaded but could not be converted to text on this machine (no pdftotext, pypdf or poppler), so only search-snippet content from it is used. Theme counts below are counts of the sources I actually saw, not a measure of how common each theme is among all caregivers. Names and health details are left out.

## 1. What caregivers complain about most when arranging care (day care, home care, nursing homes, post-hospital)

### Takeaway
The complaints that recur are nursing home cost (and uncertainty about the after-subsidy price), waiting for a subsidised bed, and crisis-mode decisions at hospital discharge. I found no first-person day care or respite complaints.

### Cited Findings
- HWZ thread "Nursing homes in Singapore, how much does it cost" (started 16 Jan 2024): the posters were planning for relatives, not giving detailed caregiving accounts. Cost estimates for a subsidised nursing home ranged from about $4k–4.5k/month before subsidy to about $1k after. The opener cited $524–$1,720/month after means testing for a grandparent placed in 2022 through a hospital referral. — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)
- Same thread, 16 Jan 2024: one poster put the wait for a nursing home bed at "around 1 month". Another doubted subsidised vacancies existed. Another projected that care costs would climb steeply over 20 years. — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)
- Same thread: one poster complained about reliance on private providers and lack of government support. Another described a named home's rooms as "so so" and probably fully booked. — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)
- Waitlist reports conflict. One forum user said about 1 month. Commercial guides say popular subsidised homes can have a waitlist of over a year (a commercial blog), or "several months to over a year" (an Aug 2026 guide). — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/); [Xiohoo guide](https://www.xiohoo.com/blog/a-comprehensive-guide-to-elderly-care-homes-in-singapore-options-costs-and-planning); [NursingHomeGuide.sg, Aug 2026](https://nursinghomeguide.sg/news/hospital-discharge-parent-cannot-walk/)
- Hospital discharge: families "often face a sudden care decision" when a parent comes home with new functional losses. This is from an editorial guide, not a first-person account. — [NursingHomeGuide.sg, Aug 2026](https://nursinghomeguide.sg/news/hospital-discharge-parent-cannot-walk/)
- Home care: costs are usually not covered by insurance or Medisave and have to be paid in cash (NUS Medicine page, undated). — [NUS Medicine, "Being a Caregiver"](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/?p=25365)
- Middle-income squeeze (OLDER, 2021): caregivers in landed property may not qualify for subsidised care. — [Malay Mail / TODAY Big Read, Mar 2021](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2021/03/06/the-big-read-as-singapore-society-ages-who-will-care-for-the-caregivers/1955474)
- Respite (OLDER, 2018, official reply): MOM said respite runs from a few hours to a month at eldercare centres and nursing homes. This was a reply to an ST Forum letter, which suggests caregivers had written in about gaps in respite. — [MOM press reply 2018](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-replies/2018/0903-range-of-care-options-is-available-to-seniors)

### Inferences
- In public forums, cost is the question asked first and most often: how much it really costs after the means test. Families appear to plan around rough figures from the internet, not a personalised estimate.
- How long the wait is depends on which home, so families get conflicting answers. A tool that shows an expected wait would answer a real question.

### Gaps
- I collected no first-person day care, home nursing or respite complaints from 2023 to 2026. The Reddit and ST Forum sources that would likely hold them were unreachable.
- HWZ thread "Anyone's family member in nursing home? Which nursing home is better?" (started 12 Feb 2023): its replies are login-gated, so I have no content from it. — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/anyones-family-member-in-nursing-home-which-nursing-home-is-better.6871752/page-2)

## 2. Not knowing what exists, being bounced between agencies, paperwork, waiting lists, confusing means testing

### Takeaway
The best-sourced theme is that caregivers do not know what exists or whom to call. The Lien Foundation's 2024 report documents it with case stories. Being bounced between agencies shows up as information not passing between AIC, MSWs and providers. I found no first-person complaints about paperwork itself.

### Cited Findings
- Lien Foundation "Care Where You Are" (Oct 2024): one caregiver profiled "needs help navigating" the eldercare system. Another did not know he could approach AIC, and kept saying he did not know whom to contact. The hospital had only given him a card listing care services. — [Lien Foundation 2024 (PDF)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf) (via search snippets; full text not extracted)
- Same report: information is not exchanged smoothly between the client, AIC, the medical social worker and frontline staff, which makes a complex system harder to navigate. — [Lien Foundation 2024](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- Same report: families do not know which agency to approach, and the process can seem "daunting and frustrating". The report cites a call for one card that brings the various schemes together. — [Lien Foundation 2024](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- "Many families do not know what an MSW can arrange," and some feel uncomfortable asking (editorial guide, Aug 2026). — [NursingHomeGuide.sg](https://nursinghomeguide.sg/news/hospital-discharge-parent-cannot-walk/)
- Means test as a black box: the HWZ opener (Jan 2024) said the price depends on "the outcome of your means testing procedure", with no way to estimate it in advance. Another poster gave the subsidy as up to 70%, while official and guide figures say up to 75–80%. — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/); [Homage guide](https://www.homage.sg/resources/dementia-care-options-singapore/)
- Some services can only be reached through one gatekeeper. For example, THK's Interim Caregiver Service takes referrals only from a hospital MSW while the senior is still in the ward, so a family that misses that window loses access. — [THK Moral Charities](https://www.thkmc.org.sg/for-the-seniors/thk-home-care-services/)
- Low-income caregivers' first contact with an MSW is often triggered by hospital bills (OLDER, 2018 NUS working paper). — [NUS CFPR Feb 2018](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/cfpr/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2020/09/Feb2018E.pdf)
- A Singapore qualitative study of unmet needs among older informal caregivers (2025, Journal of Aging & Social Policy) exists but returned 403, so I have no content from it. — [Tandfonline 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08959420.2025.2475265)

### Inferences
- Count of sources I saw, per sub-theme:
  - Not knowing what exists or whom to call: 3 (Lien x2 cases, NursingHomeGuide).
  - Information not passing between agencies: 1 (Lien).
  - Means test or cost opacity: 2 (HWZ, Malay Mail 2021).
  - Waiting lists: 3, with conflicting figures.
  - Paperwork: 0 first-person.
- The hospital stay is the moment that decides access, both through the MSW and through services available only while warded. Caregivers who learn about this after discharge lose options.

### Gaps
- I could not verify how often caregivers say they were bounced between AIC, MOH, polyclinics and hospitals. No first-person posts were retrieved.
- No first-person complaints about paperwork or repeated forms were found.

## 3. Sibling and family disagreement over cost and responsibility

### Takeaway
The theme exists in Singapore personal-finance media, but I found no 2023–2026 first-person forum posts about it.

### Cited Findings
- A DollarsAndSense story (also on Yahoo SG, date not confirmed) describes four siblings arguing over how to split a parent's hospital bill and then choosing between a nursing home and a private nurse. The suggested fix is a pooled monthly contribution. — [DollarsAndSense](https://dollarsandsense.sg/siblings-should-not-fight-over-their-parents-medical-bills/); [Yahoo SG](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/siblings-not-fight-over-parents-064019428.html)
- OLDER (2017 blog): getting siblings to share costs is called the top priority. It also mentions a $120/month grant for hiring a maid (a 2017 figure, likely outdated). — [If Only Singaporeans Stopped to Think, Apr 2017](https://ifonlysingaporeans.blogspot.com/2017/04/caring-for-aged-parents-without.html)
- HWZ (Jan 2024), helper versus nursing home: one poster said a helper is better, another replied that a "Helper can only do so much" at the end stage. This is a family trade-off debate, not a sibling conflict. — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)
- An NUS page notes that parents may disagree that they need care at all. — [NUS Medicine](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/?p=25365)

### Inferences
- Family disagreement probably comes up most around who pays and whether to hire a helper or choose a nursing home. A tool that splits costs across siblings could address it, but this is supported only weakly here.

### Gaps
- No Reddit or HWZ first-person sibling-conflict posts from 2023 to 2026 were retrieved.

## 4. What caregivers say about the AIC hotline, AIC Link, MSWs, the AIC Care Services Recommender and SupportGoWhere

### Takeaway
I found no public first-person reviews of the AIC hotline, AIC Link, the Care Services Recommender or SupportGoWhere. MSWs come up as the actual route in, both in forum posts and in a guide.

### Cited Findings
- HWZ (16 Jan 2024): a poster described applying "through the medical social worker at the community hospital" and having to shortlist a few homes. AIC was not mentioned at all in the thread. — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)
- Lien 2024: a caregiver did not know AIC was an option, which suggests low awareness of AIC as the front door. — [Lien Foundation 2024](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- SupportGoWhere's dementia day care page says referrals come from a hospital, polyclinic, GP or any AIC Link (official, not caregiver voice). — [SupportGoWhere](https://supportgowhere.life.gov.sg/services/SVC-DDC/dementia-day-care)
- AIC's own "Knowing Available Care Options" page (official) aims to make finding services easier. — [AIC](https://www.aic.sg/Caregiving-Support/Knowing-Available-Care-Options)

### Inferences
- In what I saw, caregivers name the MSW, not AIC, as the person who made placement happen. AIC appears to stay invisible to some families until someone points them to it.

### Gaps
- No Google reviews of AIC Link and no public comments on the AIC Care Services Recommender or SupportGoWhere were retrieved. Google Maps reviews are not reachable by WebFetch.

## 5. What caregivers say helped

### Takeaway
There is little first-person evidence. The help that does appear comes from people: MSWs at community hospitals, forum peers sharing cost figures, and helpers or maids for home care.

### Cited Findings
- The MSW at a community hospital handled the nursing home application (HWZ, Jan 2024). — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)
- Peer forum threads are used to crowdsource cost and wait estimates (HWZ thread itself, Jan 2024). — [HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)
- Hospital MSWs and care coordinators help families through the "bewildering array" of services and subsidies (NUS hospital page). — [NUS Medicine](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/?p=25583)
- AIC promotes caregiver support groups as a way to connect with other caregivers (official). — [AIC](https://www.aic.sg/Caregiving-Support/Knowing-Available-Care-Options)

### Inferences
- Caregivers trust people (MSWs, peers) more than portals. A navigator product may need to help them prepare for and use the MSW conversation rather than replace it.

### Gaps
- No first-person praise or criticism of Caregivers Alliance, Dementia Singapore, Club Heal or Montfort Care groups was retrieved.
