# Access and Referral Journeys for Eldercare in Singapore, and Where They Break Down

Scope note: Singapore only, current as of 24 Nov 2026. Each finding carries its data date. A lot of the "hard" waiting-time evidence is old (2016 to 2018) because MOH has not published newer day care or home care waiting times that I could find. The Lien Foundation report "Care Where You Are" is hosted at a `/2024/10/` URL path, but it was **published August 2018**, with fieldwork from June 2017 to August 2018. Treat its figures as 2015 to 2018 data.

## 1. Official referral routes per service, and how long they take (including published waiting times)

### Takeaway
Families reach subsidised services through three routes: a hospital medical social worker (MSW) or discharge planner who refers to AIC, a community or polyclinic referral, or applying directly to the provider. The latest official medians are about 1 month for a general nursing home bed (stable for 5 years as of Aug 2024) and 2 to 4 months for a psychiatric-care nursing home bed. The latest published day care and home care medians are from 2016 to 2018: about 20 days for general day care, about 35 days for dementia day care, and about 7 days for home care. MOH says respite waits "are not meaningful" because lead times vary.

### Cited Findings
- **Nursing home, general care (Aug 2024):** The median wait for a nursing home bed "has remained stable at around one month" over the past five years. Wait depends on care needs, family preferences and capacity. Source: written answer to NMP Dr Syed Harun Alhabsyi, 6 Aug 2024. — [MOH: Nursing Home Wait Time](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/nursing-home-wait-time/)
- **Nursing home, psychiatric care (Aug 2024):** The median wait is about 2 to 4 months, two to four times the general wait. The MP also asked about intellectual-disability nursing home waits, and the answer gave no figure. — [MOH: Nursing Home Wait Time](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/nursing-home-wait-time/)
- **Respite (Aug 2024):** MOH says providers have "generally been able to place" clients into nursing home respite and night respite on request. It says respite waiting times are not meaningful because caregivers apply with different lead times. — [MOH: Nursing Home Wait Time](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/nursing-home-wait-time/)
- **Nursing home (Nov 2025, unverified):** A search-engine summary attributes a 4 Nov 2025 written answer to MOH. It says the median remains about one month, and points to the Interim Caregiver Service (6 days a week, 10 to 12 hours a day) for people waiting. I could not open the primary page to confirm the date or wording. — [MOH newsroom search result: Average Waiting Time for Subsidised Beds in Nursing Homes](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/average-waiting-time-for-subsidised-beds-in-nursing-homes/)
- **Day care and home care (data 2016 to 2018):** MOH's parliamentary reply put the median wait at about 7 days for home care and about 20 days for day care. Planned centre-based respite usually starts within two weeks, and detailed respite waits were not available. — [MOH: Average Waiting Time for Seniors to Access Care Services](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/average-waiting-time-for-seniors-to-access-care-services/) (summarised in search results; I did not fetch the full page)
- **Day care by type (MOH figures given directly to the report authors, 2018):** General day care had a median wait of about 20 days and dementia day care about 35 days. There was "no wait list for home care services" at the time. — [Lien Foundation / NUS, Care Where You Are press release, 14 Aug 2018](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/CWYA_Presser_Combined_FINAL.pdf.pdf)
- **Respite application lead times (2018):** VWO day respite needed applications at least 5 working days in advance. It mostly ran until about 8pm and rarely on Sundays or for bed-bound seniors. Subsidised nursing home (planned) respite needed applications 6 to 12 weeks ahead, and "it takes about a month to process referrals." Crisis respite could admit within 1 to 2 days but was not guaranteed and **not subsidised**. — [Lien Foundation / NUS, Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **Respite lead time (current, secondary):** A consumer guide says AIC asks for respite referrals to be submitted 4 weeks before the start date, with medical documents dated within the last 6 months. — [ElderSG respite guide](https://www.eldersg.com/guides/respite-care-elderly-parents-singapore/) (secondary; not verified against AIC)
- **Interim Caregiver Service (post-discharge bridge):** It needs a referral from a hospital, community hospital or AIC. The 12 subsidised shifts run over about 2 weeks. — [Care At Homes guide (2026)](https://careathomes.com.sg/blogs/news/guide-about-interim-caregiver-service-in-singapore) (secondary)
- **Hospital-to-Home (H2H):** This is a national programme for recently discharged frail patients, or patients with complex needs, who are at risk of readmission. Care runs through home visits, calls or video for **up to 2 months** after discharge. — [SGH: Hospital to Home](https://www.sgh.com.sg/community-care/community-health-services/for-patients-recovering-in-the-community/hospital-to-home)
- **SingHealth Transitional Home Care:** A doctor, nurse and allied health team makes a home visit **within a week of discharge**, and the service can last up to 3 months. — [SingHealth: Helping patients with home care](https://www.singhealth.com.sg/news/regional-health-system/helping-patients-with-home-care)
- **CREST-PDS (dementia post-diagnostic support):** It supports newly diagnosed people with dementia and their caregivers for **up to 6 months from referral**. — [Dementia Singapore Care Services Booklet 2025](https://dementia.org.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Dementia-Singapore-Care-Services-Booklet-2025.pdf) (summarised in search results)
- **Family Service Centres:** FSC social workers provide case management and refer to care services, including day care. — [Together for Better: Caregivers](https://www.togetherforbetter.gov.sg/lifestage/caregivers/) (summarised in search results)

### Inferences
- The officially reported wait is a **median**. It hides the tail: psychiatric and dementia cases wait 2 to 4 times longer than general cases, so the families with the hardest cases also face the longest waits.
- The real time to service is the queue wait plus a front-end paperwork lag: means-testing, medical reports and AIC processing, which took about 1 month for respite in 2018. That lag does not appear in the "median wait" figures.
- Transitional programmes such as H2H (up to 2 months) and Interim Caregiver (about 2 weeks) end on a fixed date. If the long-term service (day care, nursing home) has not started by then, the family is left with a gap. This is a structural "cliff" (inference; no data found on how often it happens).

### Gaps
- **No MOH-published day care, dementia day care, home personal care, home nursing or home therapy waiting times after 2018 were found.** This is the single biggest evidence gap.
- I found no published per-step timeline for AIC Link or AIC referral processing (referral, assessment, match, start).
- I found no primary-source description of the AIC Link referral flow, or of which services accept direct walk-in applications versus requiring AIC referral, from current aic.sg pages. The AIC site was not fetched.
- I found no polyclinic-specific referral data.

## 2. MOH and AIC capacity: places planned versus available (Healthier SG, Age Well SG, AACs, nursing home and day care to 2030)

### Takeaway
Nursing home beds are rising fast, from 19,201 (2023) to 21,056 (2025), with about 10,600 more planned by 2030 (total about 31,000). Home care places also rose quickly, from 11,900 to 15,400. **Day places have been essentially flat**, at 9,200 in 2023, 9,100 in 2024 and 9,400 in 2025, and I found **no explicit 2030 target for day care or home care places**. AACs passed their 2025 target of 220, reaching 223 in Mar 2025 and about 230 or more in 2026.

### Cited Findings

  | 2024 | 20,371 | 2,553 | 9,100 | 13,900 |
  | 2025 | 21,056 | 2,579 | 9,400 | 15,400 |

  Nursing home beds by ownership in 2025: public 10,713, not-for-profit 6,137, private 4,206. — [MOH: Beds in Inpatient Facilities and Places in Non-Residential LTC Facilities](https://www.moh.gov.sg/others/resources-and-statistics/beds-in-inpatient-facilities-and-places-in-non-residential-long-term-care-facilities/)

- **Nursing home total:** Minister Ong said: "Today, we have over 20,000. By 2030 we expect 31,000." — [MOH: Remarks at doorstop on Age Well Neighbourhoods](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/remarks-by-mr-ong-ye-kung--minister-for-health-and-coordinating-minister-for-social-policies--at-doorstop-interview-on-age-well-neighbourhoods/)

- **Long-term care budget:** Raised subsidies for long-term care, including Home Personal Care, add about $2 billion to the budget from now to 2030. — [MOH: Ong doorstop remarks](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/remarks-by-mr-ong-ye-kung--minister-for-health-and-coordinating-minister-for-social-policies--at-doorstop-interview-on-age-well-neighbourhoods/)
- **Earlier growth (Nov 2021 PQ):** From 2015 to 2021, MOH added 4,600 day care places and 3,100 home care places. More than 200 centres were to move onto the Eldercare Centre model by 2024. — [MOH: Plans and Targets to Expand Day and Home Care Capacity (1 Nov 2021)](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/plans-and-targets-to-expand-day-and-home-care-capacity-to-promote-ageing-in-place/)
- **Older targets:** The 2020 targets were 10,000 home care places and 6,200 day centre places. Home and centre-based capacity grew from 5,900 places (2011) to 13,000 (2017). — [Lien Foundation / NUS, Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/CWYA_Presser_Combined_FINAL.pdf.pdf)
- **Demand versus supply signal (2015 data):** AIC received **7,800 day care referrals in 2015 when only 3,500 day care places existed**. There were 5,000 places in 2017. — [Lien Foundation / NUS, Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **AACs:** The Age Well SG goal was to grow from 119 AACs (2023) to 220 by 2025, covering about 80% of seniors. The count rose from 154 to 223 in March 2025. - **HPC+:** Enhanced Home Personal Care is to be mainstreamed island-wide by end-2025. — [MOH: Good Progress in the Implementation of Age Well SG](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/good-progress-in-the-implementation-of-age-well-sg/) (from search summary)
- **Day care providers:** More than 150 Senior Care Centres provide day care, including dementia and respite care (COS 2026, third-party summary). — [sgai.md COS MOH 2026 summary](https://sgai.md/debates/cos-moh-2026/) (third-party; not verified against MOH transcript)
- **Healthier SG:** About 1.2 million enrolled (about half of those eligible) after 1.5 years, and about 75% had completed a Health Plan consultation. — [MOH COS 2025 speech](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-ong-ye-kung--minister-for-health--at-the-ministry-of-health-committee-of-supply-debate-2025--on-friday-7-march-2025/)
- **MIC@Home (hospital-at-home):** About 200 beds as of Mar 2025, up from 104 in Jan 2024. More than 2,500 patients have used it since Apr 2024. — [Malay Mail, 13 Apr 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2025/04/13/singapore-expands-hospital-at-home-care-to-ease-bed-crunch/172921)
- **Respite capacity (old):** There were more than 450 respite places at end-2021. MOH says providers "activate" places on demand rather than holding them empty. — [MOH: Availability of Respite Care Places (11 Jan 2022)](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/availability-of-respite-care-places-for-seniors-with-short--term-care-needs_11jan2022/)

### Inferences
- **Day care is the stalled service.** Nursing home beds grew about 10% and home care places about 29% from 2023 to 2025, while day places grew only about 2%. With no stated 2030 day care target, families looking for dementia day care (already the slowest day service in 2018) face the most uncertainty. This is an inference from the MOH table. MOH may count day care growth within AAC or Eldercare Centre expansion in ways the table does not capture.
- Capacity planning is publicly target-driven for beds (hospital and nursing home) but not for community places. This matches the COS framing that nursing home shortages push seniors into hospitals.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2030 target for day care, dementia day care, home care, home nursing, home therapy or respite places.
- I found no utilisation or occupancy rates by service, so the "places available" figures cannot be compared with demand. The 2015 AIC referral figure is the latest demand-side number I found.
- I found no current respite place count after 2021.
- The COS 2026 primary transcript was not retrieved. Only a third-party summary was found.

## 3. What parliamentary questions (2023 to 2026) reveal about waiting lists, discharge delays, bed blocking, respite and caregiver support

### Takeaway
PQs show MOH tracks some things and not others. It does **not** track discharge delays caused by lack of home care arrangements, but it does count patients who are medically stable yet stay more than 21 days, **about 220 per day** (2020 to 2022 average). It reports stable median nursing home waits but declines to give meaningful respite waits. Answers point to the Interim Caregiver Service as the main bridge.

### Cited Findings
- **Bed blocking (9 Jan 2023, PQ by MP He Ting Ru):** MOH "does not track" delays due to lack of suitable home care arrangements. It does monitor patients who are medically fit for discharge but have stayed **more than 21 days**: a **daily average of 220** across public hospitals over the past three years. They are typically waiting for domestic helpers to arrive and be trained, or for admission to care facilities or services. The response was to expand the Interim Caregiver Service. No mean, median or 95th-percentile delay was given. — [MOH: Patients Delaying Discharge from Hospitals due to Lack of Suitable Care Arrangements at Home](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/patients-delaying-discharge-from-hospitals-due-to-lack-of-suitable-care-arrangements-at-home/)
- **Nursing home and respite waits (6 Aug 2024, NMP Syed Harun Alhabsyi):** The general nursing home median is about 1 month and the psychiatric median 2 to 4 months. Respite waits are "not meaningful". — [MOH: Nursing Home Wait Time](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/nursing-home-wait-time/)
- **Length of stay and the bed crunch (COS 2025):** Average hospital length of stay rose from 6 to 7 days after the pandemic, about 15% more workload. The Minister said seniors who cannot get a nursing home bed "will end up in the hospital". — [MOH COS 2025 speech](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-ong-ye-kung--minister-for-health--at-the-ministry-of-health-committee-of-supply-debate-2025--on-friday-7-march-2025/)
- **Nursing home residents' stays:** Nursing home residents average 8 days in hospital against a national average of 5. — [Frontiers in Public Health (2025), Hospital-at-Nursing-Home pilot](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1595535/full) (from search summary)
- **ED waits (2026, media):** One family reported waiting about 16 hours in the ED for a ward bed at CGH. The hospital cited high ED volume and bed occupancy. — [AsiaOne](https://www.asiaone.com/singapore/cgh-ward-bed-wait-sparks-debate-bed-crunch) (anecdote; article date roughly mid-2026 per search listing)
- **Respite reach (Jan 2026, secondary report of SMS Koh Poh Koon's PQ replies):** Subsidised respite supported caregivers of more than 14,600 seniors in 2025, plus more than 3,600 caregivers of children with disabilities or developmental needs. MOH says it is reviewing respite services. — [Batam News Asia, 15 Jan 2026](https://batamnewsasia.com/2026/01/15/social-support-policies-singapore-expands-respite-care-for-caregivers/) (secondary; primary Hansard not retrieved)
- **Caregiver support (COS 2025):** The Home Caregiving Grant increases and the Seniors' Mobility and Enabling Fund widens progressively from Jan 2026. The speech made no respite announcements. — [MOH COS 2025 speech](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-ong-ye-kung--minister-for-health--at-the-ministry-of-health-committee-of-supply-debate-2025--on-friday-7-march-2025/)
- **Caregiving plan review areas:** MOH is reviewing care navigation in the community, caregiver support including respite, and socio-emotional support. — [MOH: Updates on National Caregiving Plan](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/updates-on-national-caregiving-plan-to-address-complex-challenges-in-this-area/) (from search summary)

### Inferences
- About 220 patients a day, each staying more than 21 days after being medically fit, is roughly 1.8% of about 12,000 public hospital beds (my calculation). Because the threshold is 21 days, the true number of delayed discharges at shorter durations is higher and unmeasured.
- MOH's admission that it does not track "lack of home care arrangements" as a discharge-delay cause means the home-side bottleneck (no caregiver, no maid, no home care slot) is under-measured relative to the nursing home bottleneck.
- MOH itself lists "care navigation" as a review area, which is an implicit admission that families struggle to find their way through the system.

### Gaps
- The Hansard (sprs.parl.gov.sg) was not searched directly. PQs from 2025 to 2026 on day care waitlists, the regional distribution of waits, or rejected referrals were not found.
- I found no updated 220-per-day figure after Jan 2023.
- I found no PQ data on AIC referrals rejected by providers, or withdrawn by families.

## 4. What MSWs, AAC staff and AIC officers report as common family problems

### Takeaway
Frontline evidence (mostly qualitative, 2018 to 2025) describes the same failure points again and again: families cannot work out eligibility, there are multi-stop paperwork loops, cost shock at the provider counter causes drop-out, providers do not know how AIC matches clients, and respite is constrained by availability and affordability. Take-up after referral is low: about half in older studies, and many drop out within 6 months.

### Cited Findings
- **Paperwork loops (MSW testimony, 2018):** Patients were told to take a form to a Family Service Centre for a social report, or to a polyclinic for a health assessment, before they could apply. MSWs said patients "felt overwhelmed". — [Lien Foundation / NUS, Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **Cost-shock drop-out at intake (MSW testimony, 2018):** The MSW refers to AIC for subsidy assessment. At the centre, an administrative officer quotes the fee (for example $220 a month), the patient says "I'll just stay at home", and the case stalls unless the MSW escalates to the centre's social worker. — [Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **Opaque matching (providers, 2018):** Providers receiving AIC referrals "did not know how the agency matches clients with care providers". — [Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **Low take-up after referral (2011 to 2013 studies, cited 2018):** Only about half of 418 people referred to centre-based services used them, and take-up was similar for 322 referred to home-based services. Half of those who started had dropped out after 6 months. Reasons were perceived affordability, fear of losing independence or dignity, and doubts about formal providers. — [Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **Medisave incentive to stay in hospital (2018):** A provider said seniors think "I might as well stay in hospital", because Medisave covers hospital care but not home or community care. Up to **20% of patients turn down transitional care** because of cost or not wanting hospital contact. Transitional care costs $40 to $250 per visit and is not Medisave-payable. — [Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **Respite barriers (2025 qualitative study):** Caregivers and social workers cited availability and affordability as the main barriers to respite, especially for low-income caregivers and home-based respite. COVID-19 worsened shortages. — [Lim et al. 2025, Health & Social Care in the Community](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/hsc/8847165)
- **Older caregivers' unmet needs (2025):** Older informal caregivers put their own health second and want more frequent check-ins and flexible, person-centred support. — [Taylor & Francis, J. Aging & Social Policy (2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08959420.2025.2475265)
- **Low consent to hospital-at-home (one programme, 2020 to 2021):** 108 enrolled, and only 28% of those approached agreed. — [Annals Singapore: Treating acutely ill patients at home](https://annals.edu.sg/treating-acutely-ill-patients-at-home-data-from-singapore/)

### Inferences
- The breakdown is not only capacity. Many referrals fail at the handoff, when cost is first quoted, the next stop needs a form, or nobody owns the case between the MSW and the provider. A navigation tool that shows the fee after subsidy before the family reaches the counter addresses a documented drop-out point.
- The financing split (hospital is Medisave-payable, community care is cash) works against timely discharge. Whether later MediSave changes for long-term care fully closed this gap is not established here.

### Gaps
- I found no published AIC-officer or AAC-staff testimony from 2024 to 2026. Most frontline voices are from the 2018 Lien study.
- I found no recent (2023 to 2026) Duke-NUS CARE, IPS or SMU reports on referral failure. These were not reached within the search budget.
- There is no current quantitative data on referral take-up or drop-out rates.

## 5. How hospital discharge and transitional care work, where families fall through, and regional service gaps

### Takeaway
Discharge runs through the hospital discharge planner or MSW to an AIC referral, with bridge services in between: Interim Caregiver Service (about 2 weeks), H2H (up to 2 months), SingHealth Transitional Home Care (first visit within 1 week, up to 3 months), MIC@Home, and CREST-PDS for dementia (up to 6 months). Families fall through when a bridge ends before the long-term service starts, when the needed care is not covered or affordable, when the needed caregiver (often a newly hired domestic helper) is not ready, or when they decline. Evidence on regional (RHS-cluster) gaps was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Discharge planning meetings (2018):** Hospitals in each Regional Health System hold meetings to discuss the needs of patients about to be discharged. — [Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **Regional transitional services (2018 appendix):** NHG (Central) runs a charged personalised post-discharge service. SingHealth (East): "service begins within one week of discharge from SGH". NUHS (West) gives an individualised post-discharge care plan before discharge. — [Care Where You Are (2018), appendix](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **Why medically-fit patients wait:** They wait for domestic helpers to arrive and be trained, or for admission to care facilities. — [MOH PQ, 9 Jan 2023](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/patients-delaying-discharge-from-hospitals-due-to-lack-of-suitable-care-arrangements-at-home/)
- **Non-medical discharge delays** include insufficient nursing home capacity and difficulty arranging a caregiver at home. — [Frontiers in Public Health (2025)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1595535/full) (from search summary)
- **Transitional care outcomes:** The SGH transitional home care study (2008 to 2012) found fewer admissions and ED visits at 3 and 6 months. A Sengkang General Hospital study (2020 to 2023) examined a similar programme. — [PMC4377016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4377016); [PMC11545327](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11545327/)
- **Rehab drop-off after discharge (2008 to 2009 study, cited 2018):** 40% intended to continue rehab, 20% remained at 3 months and fewer than 5% at 1 year. This may have improved to about 50% at 3 months after Medisave was allowed for community rehab (unpublished at the time). — [Care Where You Are (2018)](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)
- **KTPH bed occupancy** fell from typically 100% or more to about 85% after new capacity. SGH opened an 80-bed acute medical ward to relieve its ED. — [MOH COS 2025 speech](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-ong-ye-kung--minister-for-health--at-the-ministry-of-health-committee-of-supply-debate-2025--on-friday-7-march-2025/)
- **New capacity in 2026:** Sengkang General and Outram Community Hospital are to add about 350 beds by converting non-clinical space. — [SGH news](https://www.sgh.com.sg/news/patient-care/subsidised-hospital-care-in-the-comfort-of-home-from-april-1-new-hospital-in-tengah-by-early-2030s) (from search summary)

### Inferences
- **Known fall-through points:**
  1. A bridge ends (Interim Caregiver after about 2 weeks, H2H after up to 2 months) before day care or the nursing home starts.
  2. The respite or nursing home application is in the system but means-testing and processing take about 1 month (2018).
  3. Crisis respite is unsubsidised.
  4. The patient declines transitional care because of cost (up to 20%).
  5. A helper is not yet available or trained.
  6. Psychiatric or dementia needs carry much longer waits.
- Hospital capacity is concentrated in the East and West through 2030 (Eastern General Hospital, Alexandra), while Tengah General Hospital comes only after 2030. Families in the North-West and West may face more pressure in the interim. This is speculative: no regional wait data was found.
