# Singapore eldercare demand and supply: statistical brief (as of 24 Nov 2026)

Legend: **[V]** = verified by fetching the primary page in this session. **[S]** = search-engine summary only; the primary page was not opened, so recheck before publishing.
Researcher scope note: 17 tool calls. Planning-area counts could not be computed because downloading the SingStat files needed permission and was not done (see Gaps).

## MOH beds and places table: all years available, all service types

### Takeaway
MOH's Singapore Health Facts table now publishes only 2023 to 2025. In 2025 there were 21,056 nursing home beds, 2,579 community hospital beds, 9,400 day places, 15,400 home care places and 3,700 home-based palliative care places. Figures for 2019 to 2022 come from parliamentary replies. The 2030 target is 31,000 nursing home beds. Day places grew only from about 8,200 (2020) to 9,400 (2025), while home care grew faster, from about 10,000 to 15,400.

### Inferences
- Nursing home beds: CAGR about 6% for 2020–2025 (16,300 to 21,056). Reaching 31,000 by 2030 needs about 2,000 beds a year, roughly double the recent pace (+1,170 in 2024, +685 in 2025).
- Day places are close to flat: +1,200 over 2020–2025, and they fell in 2024. Policy has shifted toward home care (+5,400) and AACs (+~76 centres in two years). For the tool, day-care slots are the scarcer item to help caregivers find.
- Because of the 2026 revisions, a time series must not mix pre-2026 home care numbers (e.g. 12,300 for 2022) with the revised 2023+ series (11,900 for 2023). Label the break.
- AIC Links (physical AIC touchpoints): no count found in an official source in this session.

### Gaps
- No official 2030 targets for day or home care places, and no AAC target, were found in the COS 2026 speeches checked. The Ong Ye Kung COS 2026 speech was not fetched.
- 2021 nursing home beds, and an official 2019/2022 beds series, were not verified.
- Count of AIC Links branches: not found.

## Planning-area level: senior population vs number of eldercare services

### Takeaway
No official or academic per-planning-area "seniors per eldercare service" table was found. It can be built from SingStat's June 2025/2026 resident-by-planning-area files plus data.gov.sg facility location datasets. Population Trends 2026 names Outram, Ang Mo Kio, Jurong East, Bukit Merah and Rochor as the areas with the highest share (>25%) of residents aged 65+.

### Cited Findings
- 2026: Outram, Ang Mo Kio, Jurong East, Bukit Merah and Rochor have the highest proportion of elderly, with over 25% of residents aged 65+. Tengah, Punggol and Tampines have the highest share of children under 5 — [SingStat Population Trends 2026](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/publication-resources/population-trends-2026) [S]
- SingStat publishes "Singapore Residents by Planning Area/Subzone, Single Year of Age and Sex", plus versions by age group with dwelling type or floor area, for both June 2025 and June 2026 (Excel/CSV) — [SingStat geographic distribution latest data](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/find-data/explore-data-themes/population/geographic-distribution/latest-news-data) [V]
- MOH did not answer an MP's question on the ratio of seniors to day care places (6 Jul 2023); it gave national totals only — [MOH PQ](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/ratio-of-senior-citizens-to-available-day-care-and-community-assisted-living-services/) [V]
- Academic work: Tu Yong et al. built a gravity-model accessibility and "spatial mismatch index" of ageing facilities (including AACs) against seniors by planning area, with planning areas classed as super-aged, aged, and so on, and 10-minute travel catchments — [SSRN 4487071](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4487071); [arXiv 2607.17649](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.17649) [S. The PDF could not be text-extracted here, so per-area results were not read]
- A 2026 *Urban Studies* paper found that multilingual service access for older people is concentrated in the CBD, while mature estates with over 25% aged 65+ have little — [Wang, Wang & Qi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/00420980261486152) [S]
- An older GIS (E2SFCA) study of CHAS GP access for elderly HDB residents is a usable method template — [PMC6163585](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6163585/) [S]
- Lien Foundation's "Care Where You Are" report (Oct 2024) is relevant to home and community care distribution; its content was not read in this session — [Lien Foundation PDF](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf) [S]

### Inferences
- High share does not mean a high count. Large mature towns (Bedok, Tampines, Ang Mo Kio, Hougang, Jurong West) probably hold the largest absolute 65+ counts, while Outram and Rochor are small. An under-served map should rank by **seniors per service**, not by share. This is unverified until the CSV is summed.
- Derivation recipe for the tool or case study: (1) sum ages 65+ and 80+ per planning area from the SingStat June 2026 single-year file; (2) point-in-polygon data.gov.sg / OneMap layers (eldercare services, AACs, nursing homes, CHAS clinics) against URA Master Plan 2019 planning-area boundaries; (3) compute seniors per facility and, ideally, per place (capacity is not in the location layers).

### Gaps
- Actual per-planning-area 65+/80+ counts and a "highest seniors per service" ranking were not computed: the SingStat Excel/CSV and data.gov.sg facility files were not downloaded (this needs user permission).
- No MOH or Lien analysis by planning area was found. The arXiv/SSRN per-area results were not read.

## Population ageing and long-term care demand projections

### Takeaway
21.4% of citizens were aged 65+ in 2026, and about one in four will be by 2030, with 1 in 4 of those seniors aged 80+. MOH projects about 100,000 seniors needing help with at least one ADL by 2030, and 152,000 persons with dementia by 2030 (up from about 74,000 in 2023). WiSE-2 prevalence is 8.8% of those aged 60+.

### Inferences
- The 152,000 dementia figure for 2030 implies roughly a doubling in seven years. That is hard to square with 8.8% prevalence and the 60+ population growth (about 8.8% × ~1.1M aged 60+ ≈ 100k). It may count a wider definition, such as including mild cases. Cite it as an "MOH estimate" and do not derive a prevalence from it.
- Demand vs supply in 2030: about 100,000 ADL-dependent seniors against 31,000 NH beds plus day and home places in the tens of thousands. Most ADL-dependent seniors will be cared for at home by family or migrant domestic workers. This supports a caregiver-navigation tool.

### Gaps
- Duke-NUS CARE (Centre for Ageing Research and Education) projections were not retrieved.
- The 65+/80+ year-by-year series to 2030 is not tabulated; the PiB 2026 chart values were not readable.
- The latest national count of seniors currently needing ADL help (as opposed to the 2030 projection) was not found.

## Utilisation data: respite, HCG, MediSave Care, CareShield, occupancy, waiting times

### Takeaway
Utilisation is published only sporadically through parliamentary replies. End-2020: nursing homes ~90%, day care ~52%, home care ~57%. The median nursing home wait has held at about one month (Jan 2021 and Nov 2025). Caregivers of 14,000+ seniors used subsidised respite in 2025. The HCG had 94,000+ cumulative applicants by Jan 2026.

## Healthcare workforce for eldercare

### Takeaway
MOH targets a public healthcare workforce of about 82,000 by 2030, needing about 6,000 hires a year. Another MOH framing has the total workforce growing from 129,000 (2024) to about 156,000 (2030). Foreign nurses will play a growing role. MOH says (Apr 2026) it is "on track". No community-care-specific shortfall number was found.

### Cited Findings
- 82,000 by 2030, about 6,000 nurses, allied health and support care staff a year — [MOH: annual hiring targets to 82,000 by 2030](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/annual-hiring-targets-for-healthcare-workers-to-meet-projected-size-of-82-000-by-2030/) [S]
- 129,000 (2024) → ~156,000 (2030), including growth areas such as community nurses — [MOH: Expanding healthcare capacity and transforming the workforce](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/expanding-healthcare-capacity-and-transforming-the-healthcare-workforce/) [S]
- "On track to meet our long-term manpower needs" (Apr 2026 written answer) — [MOH PQ: current workforce vs projected demand](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/current-healthcare-workforce-strength-compared-against-projected-service-demand/) [S]
- Community care workforce plans — [MOH PQ: workforce for community care](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/plans-to-ensure-adequate-and-qualified-workforce-for-community-care-services-for-ageing-population/); [MOH PQ: staff for new eldercare facilities](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/plans-to-ramp-up-supply-of-staff-for-new-eldercare-facilities/) [S, not opened]
- CCMDA 2026 (7 May 2026): more than 1,200 people upskilled since 2017; 42 nurses through the community care nursing leadership programme since 2021. No workforce size, vacancy or foreign-share figures — [MOH speech, Rahayu Mahzam](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mdm-rahayu-mahzam--minister-of-state--ministry-of-digital-development-and-information---ministry-of-health--at-the-community-care-manpower-development-awards-ceremony--7-may-2026/) [V]

### Inferences
- The two workforce targets (82k and 156k) differ in scope (public sector vs whole sector). Do not compare them.
- Workforce, not buildings, likely constrains day and home care growth. This is an inference; no official figure was found.

### Gaps
- Community care headcount, vacancy rates, and foreign worker share in nursing homes: not found.
- A search summary said "16,200 [NH beds] now", which looks like an older (c. 2020) statement. Disregard it.
