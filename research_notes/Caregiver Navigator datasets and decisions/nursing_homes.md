# Nursing homes: include in launch scope (cost-only estimate)?

Research date: 24 Nov 2026. Singapore only.
Labels: **[verified]** = I fetched the primary page and read the figure myself. **[search-summary]** = figure came from a search-engine summary or secondary blog, not checked against the primary page.

## Demand: how many seniors use nursing homes, beds, occupancy, waitlists, private vs subsidised, and how often families ask

### Takeaway
Nursing homes are a large, steady channel: about 21,000 beds in 2025, growing to 31,000 by 2030, at about 90% occupancy, with a median wait of about one month for general beds and two to four months for psychiatric beds. I found no official annual admissions figure. Family interest in cost is high: many 2026 guides on nursing home cost exist, but I found no forum threads.

### Cited Findings
- Capacity: "over 20,000 beds" now, planned "31,000 beds in 2030"; median wait for a nursing home bed "around one month", stable. Parliamentary reply to Vikram Nair MP, 4 Nov 2025 **[verified]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/reducing-average-waiting-time-for-beds-in-nursing-homes/)
- Median wait for a general care bed: "around one month" over the past 5 years. Psychiatric care bed: "around two to four months". Reply to Syed Harun Alhabsyi NMP, 6 Aug 2024 **[verified]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/nursing-home-wait-time/)
- Median wait for a subsidised bed: "around 1 month", stable, as of 6 Jan 2021. An Interim Caregiver Scheme provides custodial care "6 days a week and 10 to 12 hours a day" while families wait **[verified]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/average-waiting-time-for-subsidised-beds-in-nursing-homes/)
- Nursing home beds: 21,002 (2025), 20,371 (2024), 19,201 (2023) **[search-summary]** — [MOH Health Facts](https://www.moh.gov.sg/resources-statistics/singapore-health-facts/beds-in-inpatient-facilities-and-places-in-non-residential-long-term-care-facilities)
- Homes: 69 (2016) and 77 (2020); the 2020 split was 24 public, 22 not-for-profit and 31 private. Licensed beds: about 13,000 (2016) and 16,300 (2020); the 2020 split was 6,400 public, 6,000 not-for-profit and 3,900 private (about 24% private). Occupancy "around 90%", stable over 5 years. Reply dated 3 Aug 2021 **[verified]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/number-of-nursing-homes-and-beds/)
- End-2020 utilisation was about 90% for nursing homes, compared with 52% for day care and 57% for home care. Reply dated 10 Jan 2022 **[verified]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/current-capacities-and-utilisation-rates-of-long-term-care-services-and-plans-to-allocate-more-resources-to-nursing-homes-due-to-ageing-population/)
- Historical context: in 2013 about 500 seniors were waitlisted and the average wait was about four months. In a 2022 speech an MP claimed waits of 9 to 12 months; this is the MP's claim, not MOH data **[search-summary]** — [MOH 2013](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/waiting-time-for-admission-to-elderly-homes/)
- At Changi General Hospital, average hospital-to-nursing-home transfer turnaround fell from 72 days to 28 days after a 2019 improvement project. Hospital discharge time is longer than the MOH "median wait" **[search-summary]** — [HealthXchange](https://www.healthxchange.sg/news/faster-nursing-home-placement)
- Signs of family interest: many 2026 consumer guides focus on nursing home cost, for example [themoneybees](https://themoneybees.co/blog/nursing-homes-singapore), [carecompare.sg](https://carecompare.sg/blog/nursing-home-cost-subsidies-singapore), [seamoneytips](https://seamoneytips.com/singapore-nursing-home-cost-2026/), [smartcalculator.sg](https://www.smartcalculator.sg/articles/nursing-home-cost-singapore-2026), [Income](https://www.income.com.sg/blog/nursing-homes-in-singapore) and [Dollars and Sense](https://dollarsandsense.sg/offset-rethink-costs-putting-parent-nursing-home-singapore/) **[search-summary]**. Several of them already quote the 2026 bands, and some quote them wrongly (see Costs).

### Inferences
- About 21,000 beds at about 90% occupancy means roughly 19,000 residents at any one time. Admissions are probably several thousand a year, but that is my inference, not a published figure.
- The median wait is short, so a cost estimate is useful before placement: families must decide within weeks, often while the senior is still in hospital.
- Private homes held about a quarter of beds in 2020. Their residents get no MOH subsidy, so the tool must branch on subsidised vs private.
- The number of commercial calculators shows strong demand. It also shows that families are already being given wrong band figures, which is a reason to publish a correct one.

### Gaps
- No official count of nursing home admissions per year for 2023 to 2026. Check Singapore Health Facts or a Parliament reply.
- No 2025 or 2026 split of private vs subsidised beds; the latest is 2020. No occupancy figure after 2021.
- No psychiatric median wait after Aug 2024.
- My searches returned no HardwareZone or Reddit threads, and I have no search-volume data (Google Trends not checked). Forum demand is not evidenced.

## Costs: published fees, typical bills before and after subsidy, offsets, and means testing (including the exact 1 Jul 2026 table)

### Takeaway
The MOH subsidy table effective 1 Jul 2026 is verified from the page HTML below. Gross fees start at about $4,900 a month (AIC, Nov 2026). Ren Ci publishes bills of $620 to $3,069 a month for subsidised residents and $4,650 to $6,293 a month for non-subsidised residents. The main offsets are ElderFund ($250), MediSave Care ($200) and CareShield Life ($689 in 2026), with MediFund and ComCare as safety nets.

### Cited Findings

**MOH residential LTC subsidy table, "Effective as of 1 Jul 2026"** **[verified]**. I downloaded the raw HTML and parsed each `rowSpan`. The cells for "$900 and below" have `rowSpan="2"`, so the **$901 to $1,500 row has the same rates (80/75/50)**. The page says it was last updated 1 Jul 2026 (site footer: 23 Nov 2026) — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/subsidies-for-residential-long-term-care-services/)

|---|---|---|---|
| No PCHI | AV ≤ $21,000: 80%; AV > $21,000: 0% | AV ≤ $21,000: 75%; AV > $21,000: 0% | AV ≤ $21,000: 50%; AV > $21,000: 0% |
| $900 and below | 80% | 75% | 50% |
| $901 to $1,500 | 80% | 75% | 50% |
| $1,501 to $2,300 | 65% | 60% | 40% |
| $2,301 to $2,600 | 55% | 50% | 30% |
| $2,601 to $3,600 | 35% | 30% | 15% |
| $3,601 to $4,800 | 15% | 10% | 5% |

- Table footnotes: PCHI = total gross household monthly income ÷ number of family members living together. Gross income includes basic pay, trade or self-employed income, overtime, allowances, cash awards, commissions and bonuses. Annual Value is IRAS's estimated gross annual rent. "Subsidy levels in 2026 will be determined using 2025 Annual Values." **[verified]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/subsidies-for-residential-long-term-care-services/)
- Scope: the table covers Chronic Sick (High/Low), Nursing Home, Nursing Home Respite Care, Psychiatric Rehabilitation Home and Psychiatric Sheltered Home. It applies to SCs and PRs at **government-funded providers** only. The change was announced at Budget and Committee of Supply 2025 **[verified]** — same page
- Before 1 Jul 2026, subsidies were up to 75% (stated 17 Dec 2021). Secondary sources say the old PCHI ceiling was $3,600 and the new one is $4,800. The new table's top band is consistent with that **[search-summary for the old ceiling]** — [MOH 2021](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/health-ministry-does-not-regulate-fees-for-unsubsidised-private-nursing-home-residents/)
- Some secondary sources misstate the bands. One blog's worked example uses a $3,900 gross fee with a 60% subsidy, giving $1,560 a month. The $3,900 gross is out of date (see AIC below) **[search-summary]** — [smartcalculator.sg](https://www.smartcalculator.sg/articles/nursing-home-cost-singapore-2026)

**Gross fees and bills**
- AIC, Nov 2026: "The basic cost starts from $4,900 per month depending on the level of care needed, before any government subsidies." Deposits and other charges may apply **[verified]** — [AIC](https://www.aic.sg/care-services/nursing-home)
  - Note: earlier snippets of the same AIC page said "$3,900", and HealthHub gave $2,000 to $3,600 **[search-summary]**. AIC appears to have raised the figure when the 2026 changes took effect, so the tool should use $4,900.
- MOH estimates a Category IV (highest care) resident's fee at about $4,900 a month before subsidy **[search-summary]** — [MOF Budget 2025 Annex F-3](https://mof.gov.sg/docs/librariesprovider3/budget2025/download/pdf/annexf3.pdf)
- Ren Ci, nursing home rates effective 1 Jul 2026 **[verified]** — [Ren Ci](https://www.renci.org.sg/financial-charges/):
  - Subsidised: average daily bill $20 to $99, average monthly bill $620 to $3,069
  - Non-subsidised: $150 to $203 a day, $4,650 to $6,293 a month (includes 9% GST)
  - Bills include bed charges, drugs, special milk feeds and consumables. A one-time refundable admission deposit applies (amount not stated). MOH absorbs GST for subsidised SC and PR residents.
- MOH does not regulate fees for unsubsidised private nursing home residents (17 Dec 2021) **[verified]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/health-ministry-does-not-regulate-fees-for-unsubsidised-private-nursing-home-residents/)
- Private homes: blogs quote $3,000 to $5,000+ a month for a shared room and $4,000 to $6,500 for a single room **[search-summary, low quality]** — [themoneybees](https://themoneybees.co/blog/nursing-homes-singapore), [ExpatFocus](https://www.expatfocus.com/?p=22834)
- Anecdote: one family pays about $2,000 a month gross and about $700 after subsidy (undated) **[search-summary]** — [theAsianparent](https://sg.theasianparent.com/cost-of-care-for-elderly-in-singapore)

**Offsets**
- ElderFund: up to $250 a month for severely disabled, lower-income SCs. The nursing home can submit the Resident's Assessment Form, and payouts can go directly to the home **[search-summary]** — [CPF](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/service/article/why-is-the-elderfund-payout-250-higher-than-the-medisave-care-withdrawal-amount-200), [AIC ElderFund](https://www.aic.sg/ElderFund)
- MediSave Care: up to $200 a month for severely disabled people aged 30+ (MOH reply dated 7 Jul 2026) **[verified]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/long-term-care-financing-support-enhanced-to-improve-care-affordability/). The tiers depend on MediSave balance: $5,000 minimum, $50 a month for a $5,000 to $9,999 balance, and the $200 maximum from $20,000 **[search-summary]** — [CareShield Life FAQ](https://www.careshieldlife.gov.sg/faqs/medisave-care.html)
- CareShield Life: payout of $689 a month for claims starting in 2026, rising to $806 by 2030 **[search-summary]** — [CPF booklet 2026](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/content/dam/web/member/healthcare/documents/careshield_life_welcome_booklet_for_1979_or_earlier_2026.pdf). Older cohorts may have ElderShield instead ($300 or $400 a month); this is from my background knowledge and not verified.
- MediFund Silver (age 65+) covers bills left after subsidies, insurance and MediSave. MediFund and ComCare are the safety net after ElderFund **[search-summary]** — [AIC MediFund](https://aic.sg/Financial-Assistance/MediFund), [CareShield Life](https://careshieldlife.gov.sg/long-term-care/other-long-term-care-financing-support.html)

### Inferences
- A minimal estimate: gross bill (default $4,900, or the provider's figure) × (1 − subsidy % from the table) − CareShield/ElderShield payout − MediSave Care − ElderFund (if eligible), with a floor of $0 and the deposit shown separately. Worked check: an SC born before 1970 with PCHI $1,200 gets 80% off $4,900, leaving $980; minus CareShield $689 and MediSave Care $200 leaves about $91. This is an illustration, not a quote. It does not match Ren Ci's $620 minimum, so the tool should show a range rather than a point figure.
- The table does not apply to private (unsubsidised) beds. For those the tool can only show a market range.
- Under the birth-year column (1969 or earlier), almost every current nursing home resident falls in the higher-subsidy column, so a default of "born 1969 or earlier" is safe.

### Gaps
- No verified fee sheets for NTUC Health, St Luke's, Lions Home, Orange Valley or Econ: the search returned unrelated US results and I did not fetch these providers' pages. Ward types (for example 4-bed vs 8-bed) and care categories I to IV are not priced on any page I verified.
- I did not verify how CareShield Life, MediSave Care and ElderFund stack for a nursing home resident, or whether the subsidy is applied before the payouts.
- No official "typical bill after subsidy" figure from MOH or AIC.

## Process: how a family applies, assessment, and timelines

### Takeaway
Subsidised placement needs a referral: the family goes through a hospital or polyclinic doctor or a medical social worker (MSW), or through AIC, followed by a care assessment. AIC prioritises people who need help with three or more activities of daily living (ADLs) and have weak family support. The median wait is about 1 month for general beds and 2 to 4 months for psychiatric beds.

### Cited Findings
- To apply, families "approach their doctor at the hospital or polyclinic, or AIC" **[verified]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/subsidies-for-residential-long-term-care-services/)
- Subsidised care needs a referral and care assessment. The MSW or care team must assess that home or centre-based care cannot meet the senior's needs, and MSWs may explore home-based care first. AIC Link can help **[verified]** — [AIC](https://www.aic.sg/care-services/nursing-home)
- AIC prioritises seniors who need help with three or more ADLs and have weak family support. Subsidised placement is for SCs and PRs. While families wait, AIC arranges home and community alternatives **[search-summary]** — [ask.gov.sg AIC](https://ask.gov.sg/aic/questions/cls1kszwt00nk1codqay8pbfc), [MOH 2021](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/average-waiting-time-for-subsidised-beds-in-nursing-homes/)
- The Resident's Assessment Form is used for nursing home residents' ElderFund applications **[search-summary]** — [AIC ElderFund](https://www.aic.sg/ElderFund)

### Inferences
- The tool can give an estimate and point the family to an MSW or AIC. It cannot pre-qualify anyone, because the clinical assessment decides eligibility.

### Gaps
- I did not verify AIC's step-by-step application flow, the referral system name, or official timelines for the means-test and application steps.

## Risks: how the nursing home decision differs from home and community care, and whether a cost-only module would mislead

### Takeaway
Families describe placing a parent in a nursing home as a decision weighed down by guilt and filial-piety stigma, and public agencies frame it as a last resort after home care. A bare cost number risks reading as a nudge toward placement, and it can mislead because subsidy applies only to government-funded beds and eligibility depends on a clinical assessment.

### Cited Findings
- A first-person account: "I felt a lot of guilt" after placing his mother following her stroke, and others call such decisions "selfish or unfilial" **[search-summary]** — [Stomp](https://www.stomp.sg/stomping-ground/i-felt-lot-guilt-stomper-shares-why-he-placed-mum-nursing-home)
- NHG says institutionalisation should not be the first or only option **[search-summary]** — [NHG Health](https://www.nhghealth.com.sg/News/what-happens-when-families-can-t-cope-with-caregiving)
- AIC says MSWs may explore home-based care first. Placement requires an assessment that home or community care cannot meet the senior's needs **[verified]** — [AIC](https://www.aic.sg/care-services/nursing-home)
- Cost pushes some families to homes in Johor Bahru: "the main reason is the cost" (older Yahoo piece, undated) **[search-summary]** — [Yahoo SG](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/blogs/singaporescene/singaporeans-dying-away-home-134247626.html)
- Academic work on Singaporean caregivers' experience of placement exists (Ramanathan & Fisher, *Clinical Gerontologist*) **[search-summary, not read]** — [Taylor & Francis](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07317115.2015.1120250)
- Commercial blogs give conflicting band tables and stale gross fees (see Costs) **[search-summary]**

### Inferences
- **For inclusion (cost-only):** nursing homes are about 21k beds and growing; the decision is usually made fast, often at hospital discharge; families search for cost; the subsidy table is public and verified; and existing calculators are wrong. A correct estimate has value.
- **Against inclusion at launch:** (1) The estimate is valid only for subsidised beds, which require assessment and referral, so families may treat it as an entitlement. (2) Private home fees are unregulated and unpublished. (3) The bill range is wide (Ren Ci: $620 to $3,069 subsidised), so a single figure is falsely precise. (4) Emotionally loaded: putting a nursing home cost beside home-care options can read as "cheaper, so choose this". (5) How CareShield Life, MediSave Care and ElderFund stack is unverified.
- **Mitigations if included:** show a range, not a point; label it "subsidised beds at government-funded homes only, after MSW/AIC assessment"; show the private-home market range separately; present it beside home and community care costs without ranking them; link to AIC and the MSW; date-stamp the figures "rates from 1 Jul 2026".

### Gaps
- No Singapore study quantifying family conflict or regret after placement, and no forum threads were retrieved.
- No evidence on whether cost tools change placement decisions.
