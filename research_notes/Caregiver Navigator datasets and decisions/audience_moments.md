# Audience and entry moments for a Singapore family-caregiver planning site

Research date: 24 Nov 2026. Singapore only.
Labels: **[V]** = verified by reading the primary page/PDF directly. **[S]** = from a search-engine summary of the source; the snippet was not opened, so re-check before publishing. Derived numbers are marked **[calc]**.

## Q1. Entry moment sizes per year: which is largest, which most time-critical?

### Takeaway
Hospital admission of a 65+ parent is by far the largest and most time-critical moment: about 249,000 acute admissions of 65+ residents in 2023 (episodes, not people), with discharge planning compressing decisions into days. Dementia is the biggest *slow* moment (about 74,000 living with it in 2023, projected 152,000 by 2030). Stroke (about 9,700 cases a year) is the sharpest single trigger, since one day it is fine and the next a care plan is needed. Falls are common (1 in 3 seniors a year) but only partly lead to hospital admission.

### Cited Findings
- Acute hospital admissions, all ages: 566,790 (2021), 598,896 (2022), 620,169 (2023). Share aged 65+: 35.8%, 38.9%, 40.1%. Public acute: 452,927 / 485,723 / 504,790, of which 65+ share was 41.5% / 44.3% / 45.6%. **[V]** — [MOH Admissions and Outpatient Attendances](https://www.moh.gov.sg/others/resources-and-statistics/admissions-and-outpatient-attendances/)
- Community hospital admissions: 21,284 in 2023, 83.9% aged 65+ (2022: 23,678, 83.5%). **[V]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/others/resources-and-statistics/admissions-and-outpatient-attendances/)
- Acute admission rate, residents 65+: 334.3 per 1,000 (2025, provisional), 338.0 (2023), 335.0 (2022). Community hospital rate for 65+ was 31.1 per 1,000 in 2025. **[S]** — [MOH admission rates 2025](https://www.moh.gov.sg/others/resources-and-statistics/-healthcare-institution-statistics-hospital-admission-rates-by-age-and-sex-hospital-admission-rates-by-age-and-sex-2025/); [2023](https://www.moh.gov.sg/others/resources-and-statistics/-healthcare-institution-statistics-hospital-admission-rates-by-age-and-sex-hospital-admission-rates-by-age-and-sex-2023/)
- MOH data are "inpatient discharges" (admissions and discharges are the same series). **[S]** — [data.gov.sg admission rate dataset](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_e2acb8eaf4eaf1e0a1608af9a9cd2634/view)
- Dementia: prevalence among people aged 60+ was 8.8% in WiSE 2023, down from 10.0% in WiSE 2013 (the drop is not statistically significant). **[S]** — [PubMed: Prevalence of dementia in Singapore, changes across a decade](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39868432/)
- About 74,000 persons living with dementia in 2023, projected to reach 152,000 by 2030 (MOH parliamentary answer, Nov 2025). **[S]** — [MOH: Number of dementia-afflicted patients](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/number-of-dementia-afflicted-patients-over-five-year-period-and-future-projections-for-singapore/). Note: the 152,000 figure also appears on older pages built on a 2018 base of about 82,000, so it may predate WiSE 2023 — [NUS Design for Brain Health](https://cde.nus.edu.sg/engage/design-for-brain-health)
- Stroke: 9,702 cases in 2022, up from 5,890 in 2010 (NRDO registry, via media). **[S]** — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=234538). SingHealth gives the round figure of "9,000 new stroke cases annually" (2024). **[S]** — [HealthXchange](https://www.healthxchange.sg/news/world-stroke-organization-and-singhealth-sign-mou-to-enhance-regional-stroke-management-and-care-in-collaboration-with-angels-initiative)
- Falls: 1 in 3 people aged 65+ fall each year. Falls account for 85% of elderly trauma cases at the ED. TTSH sees about 600 hip-fracture patients a year, all aged 65+ (2012-13 data). National Trauma Registry 2012: 58% (1,448) of unintended-fall trauma cases were aged 65+. **[S, dated]** — [TTSH trauma centre](https://www.ttsh.com.sg/Healthcare-Professionals/Training-Workshops/Training-Courses/Trauma-Training-Centre/Singapore-Trauma-and-Acute-Care-Conference/News-and-Events/Pages/TTSH-s-trauma-centre-treating-more-seniors.aspx); [HPB falls CPG](https://www.hpb.gov.sg/docs/default-source/pdf/cpg_falls_preventionb274)
- Caregiver burden or burnout (moments of "we can't cope"): MOH says it does "not track caregiver burnout, but track caregiver burden" (Sep 2025). An NCSS survey (2022) found over half of caregivers "burdened" or "barely coping". A PCCERC survey (Oct 2022 to Jan 2023) found over 4 in 10 caregivers at risk of depression. An ADP study (Jul-Aug 2025, commercial) found 66% of SG workers are caregivers and 24% of them feel overloaded by stress. **[S]** — [MOH: Data on physical and mental health of caregivers](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/data-on-physical-and-mental-health-of-caregivers/); [NHG](https://www.nhghealth.com.sg/News/what-happens-when-families-can-t-cope-with-caregiving); [NTUC](https://www.ntuc.org.sg/essu/news/Challenges-faced-by-caregivers-in-Singapore-and-how-NTUC-provides-support/); [MustShareNews on ADP](https://mustsharenews.com/caregivers-workers-study-singapore/)

### Inferences
- **[calc]** 65+ acute admissions in 2023: 620,169 × 40.1% ≈ **248,700**. Public only: 504,790 × 45.6% ≈ **230,200**. These are admission episodes; readmissions mean unique seniors are fewer (no unique-person count found).
- **[calc]** Growth: 65+ acute admissions rose from about 202,900 (2021) to 248,700 (2023), roughly +23% in two years, faster than the 65+ population. The rate per 1,000 is flat (about 335), so growth comes from population ageing.
- Ranking by size per year: hospital admissions (~250k episodes) far ahead; falls (~1 in 3 of ~750k+ seniors, but most never reach hospital); new dementia diagnoses (incidence not found; prevalence 74k, rising toward 152k, implies roughly +10k a year net if the projection holds, though that projection is uncertain); stroke (~9.7k).
- Most time-critical: stroke and fall-with-fracture admissions, where discharge disposition (home with helper, community hospital, nursing home) is decided within days. That is exactly the "before they talk to the system" window, and the MSW is already in the room.
- Community hospital admissions (~21k a year, 84% 65+) are a strong sub-moment. These families are by definition facing a step-down and longer-term care question.

### Gaps
- No unique-person count of 65+ admitted per year, and no count of discharges to nursing home, home or community hospital.
- No national annual count of fall-related admissions for 65+ after 2013; no annual dementia *incidence* (new diagnoses).
- MOH 2024 admission counts were not retrieved (page snippet had no values).
- No government caregiver-burnout prevalence; the figures above use different measures and are not comparable.

## Q2. Who the caregiver is

### Takeaway
The best Singapore data (SMU ROSA, Nov 2024, ages 48-79) supports the hypothesis on the money point: **59% of caregivers manage the recipient's finances**, which is the most common caregiving task, ahead of daily care. Caregivers are mostly women (57%), often still working full-time (45%), and older than the 45-65 hypothesis suggests (one cited average age is 62). Only 39% live with the person they care for. Seniors increasingly live alone (about 88,000 aged 65+ in 2025).

### Cited Findings
- SMU ROSA brief (Mar 2025; Singapore Life Panel data collected Nov 2024, n = 7,692 aged 48-79): about **1 in 7** older adults identified as caregivers; **57.25% women**; **45.24% in full-time employment**; 76.65% care for one recipient; 54.09% of recipients are 80+; 66.09% of recipients have age-related limits such as mobility or frailty; **59.23% manage finances** for the recipient; **44.07% arrange external care services**; 44.63% give daily care; **39.45% live with the recipient**; 33.99% have 2+ chronic conditions themselves; 32.70% rate own health poor or fair; only 50.09% know about respite care, and 82.83% of those aware have never used it. **[V]** (text decoded from the PDF) — [SMU ROSA: Profiles and well-being of older adult caregivers](https://rosa.smu.edu.sg/sites/rosa.smu.edu.sg/files/Briefs/Mar25/Profiles%20and%20well-being%20of%20caregivers%20in%20Singapore.pdf)
- The same brief cites the average caregiver age as 62 years (Tan, 2023). **[V]** — [same brief](https://rosa.smu.edu.sg/sites/rosa.smu.edu.sg/files/Briefs/Mar25/Profiles%20and%20well-being%20of%20caregivers%20in%20Singapore.pdf)
- SMU media release headline: "1 in 7 older adults juggle work, chronic illness and unpaid caregiving" (Apr 2025). **[S]** — [SMU News](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/07/1-7-older-adults-juggle-work-chronic-illness-and-unpaid-caregiving)
- Seniors 65+ living alone: about 87,000 in 2024 (MOH parliamentary answer, 15 Oct 2025). **[V]** — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/data-on-seniors-aged-65-and-above-living-alone-and-plans-for-new-initiatives-to-prevent-unattended-deaths/)
- 88,400 in 2025, up from 41,200 in 2015 (about 11.5% of seniors in households), per MSF Family Trends Report 2026. 58,000 in 2018. Projected 122,000 by 2030. **[S]** — [The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/over-87000-senior-citizens-lived-alone-last-year-more-than-twice-as-many-as-a-decade-ago/); [Mothership (2030 projection)](https://mothership.sg/2024/11/122000-seniors-live-alone-singapore)
- About 40% of seniors aged 75+ lived alone or only with other seniors in 2025. **[S]** — via search summary of [MOH: Meeting the evolving care and living needs of seniors](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/meeting-the-evolving-care-and-living-needs-of-seniors/)
- Migrant domestic workers are a major part of the care arrangement: a 2022 BMJ Open study reported the average age of the care recipients of FDWs as 81, and nearly half of FDWs lacked confidence or skill for seniors' healthcare needs. **[S]** — [HealthXchange](https://www.healthxchange.sg/news/nearly-half-of-foreign-domestic-workers-lack-confidence-or-skill-to-meet-seniors-healthcare-needs-study)

### Inferences
- The "manages the money" hypothesis holds: finances (59%) and arranging services (44%) outrank hands-on daily care (45%) for this group. That matches the site's promise (cost plus who to call).
- The age band should stretch to **about 45-70**. ROSA's sample starts at 48, and the cited mean age is 62, so many caregivers are young-old themselves, some caring for a spouse. The site should not assume a 40-something user.
- With 61% not living with the recipient and the number of seniors living alone up 2x in a decade, the typical user is likely coordinating remotely (phone or WhatsApp) and often learns of the crisis second-hand, for example a call from the hospital.
- Combining 45% working full-time with the need to decide quickly at discharge makes the "one sitting" promise valuable: the user has an evening, not a week.

### Gaps
- ROSA's split by relationship (child vs spouse vs in-law) sits in figures that could not be extracted; no income breakdown found.
- No 2020-26 national (all-age) caregiver headcount; no MOM or SingStat GHS caregiver table found in this pass.
- No figure for seniors living *only with a spouse* (65+), only the 75+ "alone or with other seniors" share.

## Q3. Digital behaviour and language

### Takeaway
Seniors are largely online (smartphone ownership 89% in 2022; about 93% internet use among 60+ per an unsourced SUTD figure), and 45-65s will be near-universal. Age-split WhatsApp data and search-behaviour data were not found. English is now the top home language overall (48.3%, Census 2020), but the 65+ split was not retrieved. Caregivers rank healthcare professionals first as an information source, with the internet close behind.

### Cited Findings
- Seniors' smartphone ownership rose from 74% (2017) to 89% (2022) (IMDA Singapore Digital Society Report 2023, via C3A). **[S]** — [C3A](https://www.c3a.org.sg/node/51500); report: [IMDA Digital Society Report 2023](https://www.imda.gov.sg/resources/press-releases-factsheets-and-speeches/press-releases/2023/singapore-digital-society-report)
- "93% [internet penetration] among people aged 60 and above" (no year or source given). **[S, weak]** — [SUTD](https://www.sutd.edu.sg/news-listing/from-smart-nation-to-wise-nation-bridging-the-last-digital-mile-for-seniors/)
- Whole population: 95.8% internet penetration, 5.61M users (start of 2025). **[S]** — [DataReportal Digital 2025 Singapore](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-singapore)
- Census 2020: English is the language most often spoken at home for 48.3% of residents aged 5+. Mandarin appears to have fallen from about 35.6% (2010) to 29.9% (2020), but that extract was garbled. **[S]** — [SingStat Census 2020 infographic](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/modules/infographics/census-2020); the 65+ split is in table CT/17434 — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_ad4a8ccbdab03d16c486a9ee6988289d/view)
- Information sources: AIC's national caregiver surveys (since 2022) list healthcare professionals, the internet and AIC as key sources, and only about 41% of caregivers knew about available support services. **[S]** (journal page returned 403) — [IJIC article](https://reference-global.com/article/10.0000/up-j-ijic.11805)
- National Cancer Centre caregivers who did not search online said they trust healthcare professionals more than other sources (64%). **[S]** — [ecancer](https://ecancer.org/en/journal/article/1068-caregivers-of-cancer-patients-what-are-their-information-seeking-behaviours-and-resource-preferences)
- A 2017 Singapore study: formal sources were rated well, but family, friends and online platforms were used more often because they are convenient, helped by high smartphone ownership. **[S]** — [BMC Geriatrics](https://bmcgeriatr.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s12877-020-1454-y)
- Dementia Singapore provides resources in English, Mandarin, Tamil and Malay. **[S]** — [SUTD LKYCIC paper](https://www.sutd.edu.sg/lkycic/wp-content/uploads/sites/16/2025/09/LKYCIC-LLMAU-Webpost-Harnessing-Digital-Resources-For-DFSG-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- English-first is reasonable for a 45-70 adult-child user. Mandarin matters more if the senior is the reader. Since the senior is not the primary user, Mandarin is a second-phase need. This should be confirmed with CT/17434.
- "Healthcare professional first, internet second" supports the MSW/AAC channel. The site is a companion they can hand over, not a substitute for them.
- Low awareness (41% know support services; 50% know respite) is the gap the site fills.

### Gaps
- No IMDA age-split figures for 45-64 vs 65+ smartphone or internet use; no WhatsApp-by-age data; no 2024/2025 Digital Society Report figures found.
- No Singapore data on Facebook-group or Google use by caregivers specifically.

## Q4. The channel: MSWs, AACs, AIC Links

### Takeaway
About 940 MSWs serve public healthcare and community care (2023 and 2024), against about 230,000 public 65+ admissions a year, so roughly 245 elderly admissions per MSW a year. MOH does not track caseloads. AACs number about 214-224 and reached about 80,000 seniors in programmes (FY2023), a much bigger but less crisis-focused channel.

### Cited Findings
- About 940 medical social workers in public healthcare and community sectors in both 2023 and 2024; 2025 data not yet available; no split by setting. MOH does not track average or median caseloads (PQ for 24 Feb 2026). **[V]** — [MOH: Breakdown of MSWs](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/breakdown-of-medical-social-workers-across-public-healthcare-and-community-care-settings/)
- About 600 MSWs (FTE) in 2018. **[S]** — [MOH: Number and ratio of MSWs and AIC care coordinators](https://moh.gov.sg/newsroom/number-and-ratio-of-medical-social-workers-and-aic-care-co-ordinators-to-population-of-care-recipients-over-age-65)
- AACs: 214 as of Oct 2024 (60 added in the past year), target 220 by 2025. About **80,000 seniors took part in AAC programmes in FY2023**, up from about 17,000 in FY2021. Average daily attendance per AAC was 42 in FY2023 (17 in FY2021). Over 480,000 seniors engaged through door-to-door Preventive Health Visits since 2022. Over 800 Silver Guardian volunteers placed in AACs (target 2,400 by 2028). **[V]** — [MOH: Good progress in Age Well SG (12 Oct 2024)](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/good-progress-in-the-implementation-of-age-well-sg/)
- AACs grew from 154 to 224 since Age Well SG's launch (Nov 2023). **[S]** — [MOH: AAC-on-Wheels speech](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-tan-kiat-how--senior-minister-of-state--mddi---moh--at-the-launch-of-aac-on-wheels-/)
- AIC Links: walk-in, island-wide touchpoints for Community Care grants, services and advice. Maxwell and Outram Community Hospital sites closed 30 May 2025. Remaining sites include SGH, Alexandra, Ng Teng Fong, TTSH and Woodlands Health Campus. **[S]** — [AIC Link](https://www.aic.sg/aic-link)

### Inferences
- **[calc]** About 230,000 public 65+ admissions ÷ 940 MSWs ≈ 245 per MSW a year. Since MSWs also cover non-elderly and community cases, the real load is higher. MSWs cannot sit with every family, which is the case for a "send this link before our meeting" tool.
- AACs reach far more seniors (80k in programmes, 480k home visits) but at a calmer moment. That is the "planning ahead" channel rather than the "it happened" one.
- AIC Link consolidation (sites closing in 2025) points to fewer physical walk-in options, which favours digital pre-preparation.

### Gaps
- No MSW caseload data (MOH does not track it); no hospital-only MSW count.
- No AIC Link walk-in volume; no Caregiver Support Touchpoint count or reach; the AIC annual report was not retrieved.
- No direct evidence that families trust MSWs or AAC staff *more than websites*, only the indirect evidence above that healthcare professionals rank first.
