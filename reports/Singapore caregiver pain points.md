# Price the care before families walk away

Singapore family caregivers have three problems with eldercare. They do not know what exists. They cannot learn what it will cost them until someone quotes a fee at the counter. And the moment that decides access, usually a hospital discharge, comes before they understand the system. Only **about 41% of caregivers are aware of available support services** in AIC's 2024 survey, yet **66% of those who use services are satisfied** ([IJIC abstract](https://reference-global.com/article/10.5334/ijic.ICIC26535)). The gap is finding services, not the services themselves. Cost is the first public question and the least answerable one. The government's Care Services Recommender tells families which service type and schemes they might qualify for, but no source shows it giving a personalised out-of-pocket figure ([SupportGoWhere](https://supportgowhere.life.gov.sg/caregiving/support-recommender)). Families instead crowdsource rough numbers on forums ([HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)). The 1 Jul 2026 subsidy rise makes that gap more valuable, not less. A senior born in 1969 or earlier in a household earning $901–$1,500 per person now gets 95% off home and community care, up from 75% ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/subsidy-framework-for-non-residential-long-term-care-services/)), but most families will not know it. For a one-person, six-week, near-zero-cost project, the best first build is a cost-first estimator that stacks subsidies and grants into one monthly figure. A hospital-discharge entry flow is the strongest second layer. A caregiver-load and respite planner is worth only a light version. The current Caregiver Navigator plan is broadly right, but several 2026 facts change its tier logic, its grant figures and its action-plan wording.

## Three failures repeat: unknown options, unknown prices, decisions under time pressure

**Not knowing what exists is the best-measured problem.** Besides AIC's 41% awareness figure, SMU's ROSA panel (Nov 2024, n=7,692) found that only **50.09% of older caregivers know about respite care, and 82.83% of those who know have never used it** ([SMU](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/07/1-7-older-adults-juggle-work-chronic-illness-and-unpaid-caregiving)). The second number matters: even awareness does not lead to use. That points to friction around cost, eligibility and how to book, not only to missing information. Qualitative evidence agrees. The Lien Foundation's "Care Where You Are" study describes a caregiver who kept saying he did not know whom to contact, because the hospital had given him only a card listing services. It found that families do not know which agency to approach and find the process "daunting and frustrating" ([Lien Foundation](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)). Dementia caregiver studies in Singapore name **information** as one of four core unmet needs, alongside emotional, financial and facility needs ([Int Psychogeriatrics](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-psychogeriatrics/article/abs/perceived-unmet-needs-of-informal-caregivers-of-people-with-dementia-in-singapore/742D38FA5A4AA9118A7B81A1D4EE444B)).

**Cost shock is a documented drop-out point, not just an irritation.** Medical social workers described referring a patient to AIC for subsidy assessment. At the centre, an administrative officer quoted a fee (in one example $220 a month), the patient said "I'll just stay at home", and the case stalled ([Lien Foundation](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)). Older studies cited in the same report found that **only about half of people referred to centre-based or home-based services used them, and half of those who started dropped out within six months**. Perceived affordability was a leading reason. Up to **20% of patients turned down transitional care** because of cost, partly because MediSave covered the hospital stay but not care at home ([Lien Foundation](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)). Prices are hard to compare. Only 5 of 8 home-care providers reviewed publish any rate, and quotes mix pre- and post-subsidy, GST-inclusive and exclusive, and hourly and bundle pricing ([MissLobang](https://www.misslobang.com/article/best-home-care-eldercare-services-singapore-2026)). Families face at least four different eligibility tests. Long-term care subsidies use per-capita household income (PCHI) up to $4,800. ElderFund adds a MediSave balance test. MediSave Care uses only the balance. The Home Caregiving Grant, CareShield Life, MediSave Care and ElderFund all require help with 3 of 6 activities of daily living (ADLs), while the domestic-worker levy concession needs only 1 ADL or age 67+ ([Together for Better](https://www.togetherforbetter.gov.sg/lifestage/caregivers/); [ST via ANN](https://asianews.network/?p=305528)). Even official pages disagree. Together for Better shows two different "last updated" dates and cites 2024 Annual Values (AV, the property value IRAS sets for a home) for the grant, while MOH says 2026 subsidies use 2025 AVs ([Together for Better](https://www.togetherforbetter.gov.sg/lifestage/caregivers/); [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/subsidy-framework-for-non-residential-long-term-care-services/)).

**The hospital stay is where access is won or lost.** MOH counts about **220 patients a day who are medically fit but have stayed more than 21 days**, typically waiting for a domestic helper to arrive and be trained, or for a care placement. It "does not track" delays caused by a lack of home care arrangements ([MOH PQ, Jan 2023](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/patients-delaying-discharge-from-hospitals-due-to-lack-of-suitable-care-arrangements-at-home/)). Some bridges can only be reached from the ward. THK's Interim Caregiver Service takes referrals only from a hospital medical social worker (MSW) while the senior is still admitted ([THK](https://www.thkmc.org.sg/for-the-seniors/thk-home-care-services/)). Bridges also end on fixed dates: about two weeks for the Interim Caregiver Service and up to two months for Hospital-to-Home ([SGH](https://www.sgh.com.sg/community-care/community-health-services/for-patients-recovering-in-the-community/hospital-to-home)). Long-term services wait in queues. The median wait for a general nursing home bed is about one month, but **2 to 4 months for psychiatric care** ([MOH, Aug 2024](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/nursing-home-wait-time/)). The latest published dementia day care median, about 35 days, dates from 2018 ([Lien/NUS press release](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/CWYA_Presser_Combined_FINAL.pdf.pdf)). Families with the hardest cases therefore face the longest gap between a bridge ending and long-term care starting.

**Who is planning matters too.** The planner is often a working adult in their 50s or 60s. **45% of older caregivers still work full-time, 34% have two or more chronic conditions themselves, and 44% already "arrange external care services"** ([SMU](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/09/smu-report-nearly-14-older-adults-are-caregivers-over-half-aged-60-and-above)). About **12,300 residents were outside the labour force to care for parents in 2025**, and the male share rose from 27.8% to 40.1% since 2021 ([MOM PQ, Feb 2026](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0224-written-answer-to-pq-on-mandating-eldercare-leave)). Only about one in four caregivers who leave work return within two years ([MOM PQ, Mar 2026](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0302-written-answer-to-pq-on-time-taken-for-caregivers-to-return-to-workforce)). A good plan made early has real economic value, because the alternative is often quitting.

## Existing tools stop exactly where the cost question begins

Government information is spread across aic.sg, SupportGoWhere, Singapore Silver Pages and its E-Care Locator, HealthHub, LifeSG, hospital-cluster apps and a weekday hotline. Each covers one slice ([ask.gov.sg](https://ask.gov.sg/aic/questions/cls1kszwl00hs1codej46s2tl); [GovTech](https://tech.gov.sg/media/technews/how-tech-can-relieve-caregiver-burnout); [aic.sg](https://aic.sg/)). The Care Services Recommender answers "which service and which schemes might fit". Its service pages say "fees apply" and "means-tested" and do not give a personalised figure ([SupportGoWhere Nursing Home](https://supportgowhere.life.gov.sg/services/SVC-NH/nursing-home)). Applications run elsewhere. The Home Caregiving Grant needs a Functional Assessment Report (FAR) first and is then applied for on AIC's eFASS portal ([NTUC Health](https://ntuchealth.sg/elderly-care/resources/home-care/how-to-apply-for-the-home-caregiving-grant-for-care-at-home)). Private platforms such as Homage and Jaga-Me sell their own supply and quote unsubsidised rates ([Homage](https://www.homage.sg/services/elderly-care/)). Active Global still listed the grant at $200 a month after it rose to $600 ([MissLobang](https://www.misslobang.com/article/best-home-care-eldercare-services-singapore-2026)), which shows how fast third-party figures go stale. Australia's My Aged Care is the benchmark. It puts a provider finder, side-by-side comparison and a government fee estimator in one place ([My Aged Care](https://myagedcare.gov.au/find-a-provider); [Fee estimator](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/news-and-updates/spotlight-my-aged-cares-fee-estimator)). Singapore has no equivalent.

The government is moving toward **human** navigation, not a consumer calculator. Seniors have received one standardised care assessment since April 2026. From **October 2026** they progressively get a single community care plan coordinated by Integrated Community Care Providers (ICCPs) across 84 sub-regions ([Age Well SG](https://www.agewellsg.gov.sg/care-support/); [MOH COS 2026](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-tan-kiat-how--senior-minister-of-state--ministry-of-digital-development-and-information---ministry-of-health--at-the-ministry-of-health-committee-of-supply-debate-2026--on-5-march-2026/)). **Caregiver Support Touchpoints** open from 2027 at five aged-care service centres and three Enabling Services Hubs. They will identify needs and connect caregivers to support, and the announcement mentions no digital tool ([MOH reply, Oct 2026](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/outreach-to-identify-isolated-informal-caregivers-of-senior-citizens/)). These reforms help families *after* they enter the system. The self-serve phase before any referral stays open, when a family is searching at night and has not yet reached anyone. The realistic threat is a future version of the Care Services Recommender that adds a cost estimate, or a Budget 2027 announcement (due Feb 2027). Either should be treated as moderately likely over a two-year horizon, not as imminent.

Caregivers also trust people more than portals. In the forum and report evidence, the MSW, not AIC, is who actually made placement happen. One HardwareZone poster applied "through the medical social worker at the community hospital" and never mentioned AIC ([HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)). A guide notes that "many families do not know what an MSW can arrange" ([NursingHomeGuide.sg](https://nursinghomeguide.sg/news/hospital-discharge-parent-cannot-walk/)). The product should therefore prepare families for the MSW, AIC Link or ICCP conversation, not try to replace it.

## Three product directions, judged on evidence and six-week feasibility

The table compares the three options. The detailed write-up of each follows.

| | A. Net-cost estimator | B. Discharge countdown | C. Caregiver-load planner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core pain | Cost shock, means-test black box | Ward-only access, bridge cliffs | Respite non-use, job exit |
| Evidence strength | Strong (Lien drop-out, CSR gap, forums, scheme complexity) | Medium (MOH 220/day, ward-gated services; no measure of how often bridges end with no long-term care in place) | Medium (ROSA respite, MOM job exit), weaker link to a tool |
| Build risk in 6 weeks | Low to medium (rule tables) | Low (dated checklist) | Medium (hours model, emotional content) |
| Gov't overlap risk | Medium (CSR could add costs) | Low to medium (ICCP plans start after entry) | Medium (CSTs from 2027) |
| Verdict | **Build first, as the core** | **Build as second entry mode** | Fold a light version into A |

### A. "What will this really cost us?" net-cost estimator

**Problem.** Families cannot see their out-of-pocket monthly cost until a provider quotes it, and the quote is where referrals stall. **Evidence.** There is the Lien MSW testimony on the $220 quote and drop-out, plus half of referred people never using services and up to 20% declining transitional care over cost ([Lien Foundation](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Care-Where-You-Are-FINAL-Report-pdf-2.pdf)). Forum users guess post-subsidy costs and cite subsidy rates that are wrong ([HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)). The Care Services Recommender offers no personalised cost, and four overlapping eligibility tests sit across schemes. **Who.** The working adult child, aged 45–65, who is the "finance manager" for a parent (59% of older caregivers manage the recipient's finances, per [SMU](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/07/1-7-older-adults-juggle-work-chronic-illness-and-unpaid-caregiving)).

**Core flow.** The caregiver answers six questions: residency and birth year, household size and income band, home type, ADL count, care needs, and whether there is a domestic helper. They tick the services in a weekly plan. The tool shows a **monthly range** for each service: list price, minus the 1 Jul 2026 subsidy, plus transport. It then shows a second column of offsets the family *may* be able to claim: Home Caregiving Grant ($600/$400/$200), MediSave Care (up to $200), CareShield Life ($689 in 2026), ElderFund ($250/$150) and the $60 helper levy concession. The result is one net figure with each line labelled "likely / possible / unlikely, confirm with AIC".

The numbers explain why this matters. At the NTUC midpoint of about $1,350 a month for day care, a household earning ≤$1,500 per person pays about **$68** at 95% subsidy. One earning $3,601–$4,800 pays about **$878** at 35% (illustrative arithmetic on [NTUC Health](https://ntuchealth.sg/elderly-care/elderly-care-faq) fees and [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/subsidy-framework-for-non-residential-long-term-care-services/) rates, excluding transport and GST). For the lowest band, a $600 grant can exceed the subsidised day care fee. The real barrier there is knowing about the grant and the 3-ADL gate, not price. For the top band, the cost is real and the grant falls to $200. MOH's own benchmark, about **$110 a month out of pocket** for a low-income senior with severe disability using typical home and community services with CareShield Life ([MOH, Jul 2026](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/long-term-care-financing-support-enhanced-to-improve-care-affordability/)), is a ready test case.

**Deliberately does not.** It does not decide eligibility, compute exact PCHI or AV, quote any centre's actual fee, store inputs, or advise on finances. Its output says "estimate", gives the date of the rule tables, and points to AIC's 1800 650 6060 or the MSW for the formal means test. **Data and feasibility.** The MOH subsidy tables and the Together for Better grant tables are small enough to hard-code as dated JSON. Price ranges come only from NTUC Health's published fee page plus the MOH benchmark, so ranges are honest and points are not possible. This fits a static site and six weeks, and roughly two weeks of the effort goes into rule encoding and test cases. **Risks.** A wrong number does more harm than no number, so every rule needs a unit test against hand-worked cases. Tables change at each Budget or Committee of Supply debate, and stale figures are the exact failure the tool criticises. If the Care Services Recommender adds cost estimates, this becomes a portfolio piece rather than a live public tool. That is acceptable for the stated goal. **Success.** Every encoded tier reproduces hand-calculated cases and MOH's $110 benchmark. In five usability sessions, caregivers can state a plausible monthly figure and the next phone call to make within five minutes. They also rate the estimate as more useful than "fees apply". **Verdict: build first.** It has the strongest evidence, the clearest gap, and it is the existing plan's cost engine made sharper.

### B. "Discharge countdown": what to ask before Mum leaves the ward

**Problem.** Families make the biggest decisions while a parent is in hospital, and some options are only reachable from there. Short bridges then end before long-term care starts. **Evidence.** About 220 medically fit patients a day have stayed more than 21 days, waiting for a helper or a placement ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/patients-delaying-discharge-from-hospitals-due-to-lack-of-suitable-care-arrangements-at-home/)). Interim Caregiver Service referrals come only from the ward MSW ([THK](https://www.thkmc.org.sg/for-the-seniors/thk-home-care-services/)). Bridge services run about two weeks, up to two months for Hospital-to-Home, and up to three months for SingHealth Transitional Home Care ([SingHealth](https://www.singhealth.com.sg/news/regional-health-system/helping-patients-with-home-care)). Psychiatric nursing home waits are 2–4 months. Respite referrals are reported to need about four weeks' lead time ([ElderSG](https://www.eldersg.com/guides/respite-care-elderly-parents-singapore/), secondary). An SGH study found caregivers may be unprepared for care needed after discharge ([PMC6875807](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6875807)). **Who.** A child who has just heard "your father can be discharged on Friday".

**Core flow.** The caregiver enters the expected discharge date and picks the senior's situation (can walk or not, dementia, helper coming or not). The tool produces a dated timeline: what to ask the MSW *today* (Interim Caregiver Service, Hospital-to-Home, a FAR for the grant, a referral for day care or nursing home), when each bridge likely ends, and the overlap or gap against typical waits. It ends with a one-page "questions for the MSW" sheet to print or WhatsApp to siblings. **Deliberately does not.** It does not promise placement, give real-time waitlists, or contact any hospital. It frames waits as published medians with their dates. **Data and feasibility.** Static rules from MOH replies and hospital programme pages, so it is cheap to build (about one and a half weeks on top of A, reusing A's plan and sharing output). **Risks.** Waiting-time data for day and home care is 2016–2018 vintage. Hospital and cluster practices differ. The ICCP single care plan from October 2026 may make MSW-to-ICCP handover smoother and shrink the cliff. Nobody has measured how often a bridge ends with no long-term care in place, so the size of the problem is not known. **Success.** In tests with caregivers who have been through a discharge, and ideally one MSW, at least one question on the sheet is judged "something I wish I'd asked". The timeline is checked as plausible by someone who knows the process. **Verdict: build as the second entry mode.** It targets the most time-critical moment with the least code, and it puts the "prepare for the MSW" insight into practice.

### C. "Before you quit": caregiver-load and respite planner

**Problem.** Caregivers carry hours they cannot sustain, rarely use respite even when they know about it, and leave jobs they seldom regain. **Evidence.** 82.83% of respite-aware caregivers have never used it ([SMU](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/07/1-7-older-adults-juggle-work-chronic-illness-and-unpaid-caregiving)). About 6 in 10 full-time employed caregivers give 20+ hours a week ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0506-written-answer-to-pq-on-employed-caregivers-who-provide-substantial-informal-care)). About 12,300 people were out of work to care for parents in 2025, and only about one in four return within two years. Caregivers and social workers name availability and affordability as respite barriers ([Lim et al. 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/hsc/8847165)). **Who.** An employed caregiver weighing reduced hours or resignation.

**Core flow.** The caregiver maps the week's care hours by who covers them. The tool shows which uncovered hours day care, home personal care, enhanced home personal care with respite (HPC+, from April 2026; [MOH COS 2026](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-tan-kiat-how--senior-minister-of-state--ministry-of-digital-development-and-information---ministry-of-health--at-the-ministry-of-health-committee-of-supply-debate-2026--on-5-march-2026/)), planned respite or a helper could take, with A's cost attached. It suggests booking respite well ahead. **Deliberately does not.** It does not screen for depression or give mental-health advice. A short burden screen such as ZBI-9 is tempting, but it adds clinical-safety duty a solo project cannot carry. It does not give employment or legal advice. **Data and feasibility.** It reuses A's data and adds an hours grid. That is technically feasible, but the value depends on content and testing that six weeks will not cover well. **Risks.** Caregiver Support Touchpoints from 2027 are designed for exactly this conversation. The 83% non-use may come from stigma or reluctance, which a tool cannot fix. **Success.** The share of test users who add at least one respite or day-care block they had not planned. **Verdict: do not build separately.** Fold it into A as the weekly plan grid, plus a "you've covered X of Y hours" line and a respite-lead-time nudge.

The sibling cost-split idea is real but weakly sourced, from one personal-finance story and an old blog ([DollarsAndSense](https://dollarsandsense.sg/siblings-should-not-fight-over-their-parents-medical-bills/)). It is a cheap add-on to A's WhatsApp output (net cost ÷ number of siblings), not a direction of its own.

## 2026 facts that change the current design

**Subsidy tiers T0 and T1 merge.** In MOH's raw table HTML, the 95/80/55% cells for "$900 and below" use `rowSpan="2"`, so they also cover **$901–$1,500**. Scraped versions make that row look empty. The COS booklet confirms maximum subsidy now reaches $1,500 PCHI ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/subsidy-framework-for-non-residential-long-term-care-services/); [COS 2025 booklet](https://file.go.gov.sg/mohcos2025booklet.pdf)). The home and community tiers are therefore: ≤$1,500 or no income with AV ≤$21,000 (95/80/55), $1,501–$2,300 (85/70/45), $2,301–$2,600 (75/60/35), $2,601–$3,600 (55/40/20), $3,601–$4,800 (35/20/10), and above $4,800 or no income with AV >$21,000 (0%). The residential (nursing home) table has its own rates, 80/75/50 at the top band, and its $901–$1,500 row is inferred by analogy rather than checked in its own HTML. Subsidy does not depend on ADL count ([MOH COS 2026](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-tan-kiat-how--senior-minister-of-state--ministry-of-digital-development-and-information---ministry-of-health--at-the-ministry-of-health-committee-of-supply-debate-2026--on-5-march-2026/)), so the ADL question drives grants, not subsidy. Anyone born after 1969 is 56 or younger in 2026, so for most seniors the "born 1969 or earlier" citizen column is the one that applies. The questionnaire can ask residency plus birth year instead of a separate cohort question.

**Home Caregiving Grant tiers are $600 / $400 / $200 from April 2026.** The bands are $600 at PCHI $0–$1,500 (or no income with AV ≤$21,000), $400 at $1,501–$3,600, and $200 at $3,601–$4,800, with $200 for owners of multiple properties. The grant needs help with 3 or more ADLs, the senior must not live in a residential facility, and it requires a FAR followed by an eFASS application ([Together for Better](https://www.togetherforbetter.gov.sg/lifestage/caregivers/); [NTUC Health](https://ntuchealth.sg/elderly-care/resources/home-care/how-to-apply-for-the-home-caregiving-grant-for-care-at-home)). MOH kept the 3-ADL line in August 2026 ([SG Parliament](https://www.sgparliament.com/debate/33340)). Other figures to encode are CareShield Life at $689 a month in 2026, rising 4% a year ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/government-accepts-careshield-life-council-s-recommendations-to-enhance-careshield-life--provides-additional--570-million-to-subsidise-premiums/)), MediSave Care capped at $200, and ElderFund at $250/$150 ([ST via ANN](https://asianews.network/?p=305528)). The Seniors' Mobility and Enabling Fund now includes PRs and runs to $4,800 PCHI ([Together for Better](https://www.togetherforbetter.gov.sg/lifestage/caregivers/)).

**From October 2026 the action plan should point to one assessment and one care plan.** The single community care plan through ICCPs rolls out progressively from October 2026, and the standardised assessment has applied since April 2026 ([Age Well SG](https://www.agewellsg.gov.sg/care-support/)). Telling families to apply separately for each service is now partly outdated. The plan's next step should read "ask for the single assessment / care plan via the MSW, GP or AIC Link". The printout is then a pre-read for that conversation, and the tool shows a "your area may already be on the new single care plan" note.

**Caregiver Support Touchpoints from 2027 belong in the output as a future route.** They give the plan a second human route besides AIC Links. They also signal where government will compete. Keep the tool's caregiver-support section short and link out ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/outreach-to-identify-isolated-informal-caregivers-of-senior-citizens/)).

**Smaller changes.** The flow needs a helper branch, because helpers often deliver the plan and the levy concession applies at 1 ADL or age 67+ ([MOM](https://mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-domestic-worker/foreign-domestic-worker-levy/levy-concession)). Day care transport ($250–$440 before subsidy) is a separate line ([NTUC Health](https://ntuchealth.sg/elderly-care/elderly-care-faq)). A dementia option must show longer expected waits. A discharge flag must surface the services that can only be reached from the ward. Provider locations can come from data.gov.sg's eldercare dataset ([data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/dataset/eldercare-services)), but its coverage of AIC Links and how current it is still need checking.

## What this evidence cannot carry

**First-person evidence is thin.** Reddit could not be fetched, and HardwareZone replies are partly behind a login. Almost all "public voice" comes from one 2024 HardwareZone cost thread, a few editorial guides, and case stories from the Lien report. Theme counts are counts of sources seen, not prevalence. Nothing first-person was found on day care, respite, paperwork, the AIC hotline, AIC Link or the Care Services Recommender ([HardwareZone](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/nursing-homes-in-singapore-how-much-does-it-cost.6990952/)). This is why week 1 has to include real conversations.

**Several headline figures are [S], meaning taken from search summaries and not read on the source page.** These include AIC's 41% awareness and 66% satisfaction (a conference abstract, with sample size unknown), Duke-NUS's caregiver prevalence of 6% rising to 7.4%, the $1.28 billion and $1,745-a-month care valuations, 87,100 people outside the labour force for caregiving, and the "one in four return within two years" figure. The ROSA figures and MOM's 12,300 parent-carer count were read on the source page. Use the [S] figures in pitch material only with "reported" wording until they are checked.

**Waiting-time data is old.** No MOH-published day care, dementia day care or home care waits after 2016–2018 were found. Only nursing home medians are current (Aug 2024). A secondary search result pointed to a November 2025 reply, but it could not be opened. No regional waiting data exists at all, so any "expected wait near you" feature would be invented ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/average-waiting-time-for-seniors-to-access-care-services/)). Capacity data adds a warning. Day places were flat at 9,200 / 9,100 / 9,400 for 2023–2025, while nursing home beds and home care places grew ([MOH capacity table](https://www.moh.gov.sg/others/resources-and-statistics/beds-in-inpatient-facilities-and-places-in-non-residential-long-term-care-facilities/)). Old day care waits may understate today's.

**The Lien Foundation report's date is confused across the notes.** "Care Where You Are" is hosted under a `/2024/10/` URL, and two research notes cite it as an October 2024 report. Its press release is dated **14 Aug 2018**, and its fieldwork ran June 2017 to August 2018 ([press release](https://lienfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/CWYA_Presser_Combined_FINAL.pdf.pdf)). This report treats it as 2018 evidence. The drop-out, paperwork and "did not know whom to contact" stories are therefore pre-COVID and come before Healthier SG, Age Well SG, AACs, ICCPs and the 2026 subsidy rise. They show the failure modes but not their current size. Its full text was never extracted, so some points rest on snippets.

**Cost evidence is list-price only.** NTUC Health is the only provider publishing a broad fee schedule, and its low-end figures are probably already post-subsidy. No survey of what families actually pay after July 2026 exists. MOH and AIC publish no net-fee tables. The estimator can honestly give ranges, not quotes.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves the project's centre of gravity. "Find a provider" is the part government already does reasonably well. The neglected step is the one between "this service might fit" and "we can afford it and know who to call". That step is where 2018-era referrals died, and the July 2026 subsidy rise made it more relevant: many households are now far better off than they assume, and a few are worse off than they hope. Build the net-cost estimator (A) as the core in weeks 1–4, with C's weekly hours grid as its input. Add the discharge countdown (B) as a second entry mode in weeks 5–6. Make the printable/WhatsApp output a brief for the MSW, AIC Link or ICCP conversation rather than a substitute for it.

In week 1, validate four things before writing UI. First, encode the 1 Jul 2026 subsidy and grant tables as dated JSON with unit tests: the merged ≤$1,500 tier, the HCG bands, and MOH's ~$110 benchmark as a regression case. Second, walk through the live Care Services Recommender by hand to confirm it gives no personalised cost. If it does, pivot to B as the lead. Third, check that data.gov.sg's eldercare dataset includes AIC Links and is current enough to map. Fourth, hold three to five short conversations with caregivers who arranged care in the past two years, plus one MSW or AAC staffer if reachable. Test two claims: "seeing a net monthly figure early would have changed what we did", and "I'd have asked different questions in the ward". If neither lands, the thin first-person evidence was misleading, and it is better to learn that in week 1 than in week 6.
