# Start at the ward, curate the rest

The Caregiver Navigator's user is confirmed with one revision: it is the adult child who manages the parent's money, but the age band should run **45 to 70, not 45 to 65**, because Singapore's best caregiver panel finds the average caregiver is 62 and that **59% of older caregivers manage the recipient's finances**, more than give daily care ([SMU ROSA](https://rosa.smu.edu.sg/sites/rosa.smu.edu.sg/files/Briefs/Mar25/Profiles%20and%20well-being%20of%20caregivers%20in%20Singapore.pdf)). The entry moment that matters most is a hospital admission: about **249,000 acute admissions of residents aged 65+ in 2023**, against roughly 9,700 strokes and 74,000 people living with dementia ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/others/resources-and-statistics/admissions-and-outpatient-attendances/)). Strokes and fall fractures mostly arrive through that same hospital door, so the site should ask "Where is Mum right now?" first and route on the answer. The channel that meets these families is thin: about **940 medical social workers** serve all of public healthcare and community care ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/breakdown-of-medical-social-workers-across-public-healthcare-and-community-care-settings/)), or roughly 245 elderly public admissions per MSW a year. The data picture is the bigger surprise. The rules and prices the product depends on exist only as web pages, and the provider data is weaker than the plan assumed. data.gov.sg's "Eldercare Services" layer was last edited in **July 2016** and holds 133 points with pre-2022 names. There is no open dataset for Active Ageing Centres, AIC Links, nursing homes or day care, and no machine-readable subsidy rule anywhere ([data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_f0fd1b3643ed8bd34bd403dedd7c1533/view)). So the build should hand-curate a short, dated "who to call" list, hard-code the verified 1 Jul 2026 subsidy tables, include nursing homes only as a labelled cost range, call OneMap's token-free postal search directly with a fallback instead of running a serverless proxy, and keep the planning-area analysis as a case-study chart rather than a feature.

Status labels used throughout: **[V]** means the researcher read the figure on the primary page or in the data itself on 24 Nov 2026. **[S]** means it came from a search summary or a secondary site and needs a manual check before publishing. **[calc]** marks arithmetic done on cited figures.

## The money-managing child is older than assumed, and usually not at home

The "adult child who manages the money" hypothesis holds on the strongest Singapore data available. SMU's ROSA brief (Singapore Life Panel, Nov 2024, n = 7,692 aged 48 to 79) finds that about 1 in 7 older adults are caregivers. Of those caregivers, **59.23% manage the recipient's finances**, 44.07% arrange external care services and 44.63% give daily care. **57.25% are women and 45.24% work full-time** [V] ([SMU ROSA](https://rosa.smu.edu.sg/sites/rosa.smu.edu.sg/files/Briefs/Mar25/Profiles%20and%20well-being%20of%20caregivers%20in%20Singapore.pdf)). Money and arranging care are this group's main jobs, which matches the site's promise of cost plus who to call.

The age band is where the evidence pushes back. The same brief cites an **average caregiver age of 62**, and SMU's release reports over half of these caregivers are aged 60 and above ([SMU News](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2025/04/09/smu-report-nearly-14-older-adults-are-caregivers-over-half-aged-60-and-above)). **54% of care recipients are 80+** [V]. A parent of 85 to 95 has children in their late 50s to late 60s. A 45 to 65 band would cut off a large share of real users, many of whom are managing their own chronic illness: **34% have two or more chronic conditions and 33% rate their own health as fair or poor** [V]. The recommendation is to widen the stated band to **45 to 70** and design the persona around a working 55 to 65 year old, with type size, contrast and plain language set for the older end. One caveat: ROSA starts at age 48 and its split by relationship (child, spouse, in-law) sat in figures that could not be extracted, so the share of these caregivers who are adult children rather than spouses is not known.

Two more facts shape the design more than age does. **Only 39.45% of caregivers live with the person they care for** [V], and seniors living alone doubled in a decade to about **87,000 in 2024** ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/data-on-seniors-aged-65-and-above-living-alone-and-plans-for-new-initiatives-to-prevent-unattended-deaths/)) [V]. The typical user is coordinating at a distance and often hears about the crisis second-hand, from a hospital call or a neighbour. That user has an evening, not a week, which is why "one sitting" is the right promise. Awareness is the gap the site fills. Only **50% of caregivers know respite care exists, and 83% of those who know have never used it** [V].

### The human channel is small and stretched

**About 940 MSWs** work across public healthcare and community care in both 2023 and 2024, and MOH does not track their caseloads [V] ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/breakdown-of-medical-social-workers-across-public-healthcare-and-community-care-settings/)). Against about 230,000 public 65+ admissions, that is roughly **245 elderly admissions per MSW a year** [calc], and the true load is higher because MSWs also serve younger and community cases. MSWs cannot sit with every family, so a link they can send before the meeting is useful to them as well as to families. Active Ageing Centres are a much wider but calmer channel. There were **214 AACs in Oct 2024** reaching about 80,000 seniors in programmes in FY2023 ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/good-progress-in-the-implementation-of-age-well-sg/)) [V], and **over 230 by Mar 2026 serving about 100,000 seniors** ([COS 2026](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mr-tan-kiat-how--senior-minister-of-state--ministry-of-digital-development-and-information---ministry-of-health--at-the-ministry-of-health-committee-of-supply-debate-2026--on-5-march-2026/)) [V]. AACs suit a "planning ahead" message, not the crisis. Physical AIC Links are shrinking: the Maxwell and Outram Community Hospital sites closed on 30 May 2025, leaving sites at SGH, Alexandra, Ng Teng Fong, TTSH and Woodlands Health Campus [S] ([AIC Link](https://www.aic.sg/aic-link)). Fewer walk-in options make digital preparation more valuable.

### Who it is not for

The site is not for the senior as the main reader. Seniors are online (89% own smartphones [S], [C3A](https://www.c3a.org.sg/node/51500)), but a senior reader raises the Mandarin, Malay and Tamil need that an English-first adult-child tool can defer. It is not for families who need live vacancies, provider booking or an eligibility ruling, because no open data supports vacancies and eligibility is set by a clinical assessment and means test. Its cost engine adds little for households above **$4,800 per-capita income**, which get 0% subsidy (the ceiling covers about 7 in 10 households, so roughly 3 in 10 are above it [V]), for foreigners without SC or PR status, and for families choosing private-only care, though the who-to-call part still serves them. It is not for palliative or end-of-life planning, for disability care among under-65s, or for families already inside an ICCP single care plan, who have a human navigator. It gives no clinical, financial-product or legal advice.

## Six open decisions, settled on the evidence

### (a) Ask "Where is Mum right now?" first: yes

The case for asking first is strong. The hospital moment is the largest by an order of magnitude and the most time-critical, and some services can only be reached from the ward. THK's Interim Caregiver Service takes referrals only from a hospital MSW while the senior is admitted ([THK](https://www.thkmc.org.sg/for-the-seniors/thk-home-care-services/)). MOH counted about **220 medically fit patients a day staying more than 21 days**, typically waiting for a helper or a placement ([MOH PQ, Jan 2023](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/patients-delaying-discharge-from-hospitals-due-to-lack-of-suitable-care-arrangements-at-home/)). A family that does not know it is in the hospital branch asks the wrong questions while the window is open. The question is also easy to answer under stress, unlike income or ADL counts. The case against is that it adds a step before the cost figure, which is the site's core value, and it frames everything around a crisis when some dementia families are planning slowly. The resolution is to make it one tap with three answers (in hospital or community hospital; at home and something has just changed; at home and we're struggling), let it set the timeline, flags and who-to-call list, and keep the cost questions that follow identical across branches. A fourth answer, "already in a nursing home", should route to the residential cost range and offsets only.

### (b) Include nursing homes as a cost-only range: yes, with guard rails

For inclusion: nursing homes are a large and growing channel at **21,056 beds in 91 homes in 2025** [V] ([data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_74ee2734189f4124fefbf01c3fe48bec/view)), heading to 31,000 by 2030, at about 90% occupancy. The median wait is about **one month for a general bed and 2 to 4 months for a psychiatric bed** [V] ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/nursing-home-wait-time/)), so the cost question arrives while the parent is still in hospital. Families already search for it, and the calculators they find are wrong: one widely indexed 2026 guide works from a stale $3,900 gross fee [S] ([smartcalculator.sg](https://www.smartcalculator.sg/articles/nursing-home-cost-singapore-2026)), while AIC now says **"starts from $4,900 per month"** before subsidy [V] ([AIC](https://www.aic.sg/care-services/nursing-home)). The residential subsidy table is now verified, including the row the earlier research had only inferred. In the raw HTML of the residential page the "$900 and below" cells carry `rowSpan="2"`, so **$901 to $1,500 gets the same 80/75/50%** ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/subsidies-for-residential-long-term-care-services/)) [V].

Against inclusion: the table applies only to government-funded beds after an MSW or AIC assessment finds that home care cannot meet the need, so a figure can read like an entitlement. Private homes, about a quarter of beds in 2020, have unregulated, mostly unpublished fees ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/number-of-nursing-homes-and-beds/)). Real bills vary widely: Ren Ci's subsidised residents pay **$620 to $3,069 a month** and non-subsidised residents $4,650 to $6,293 [V] ([Ren Ci](https://www.renci.org.sg/financial-charges/)). How CareShield Life, MediSave Care and ElderFund stack on a nursing home bill is unverified. Placement also carries guilt and filial-piety weight, and NHG says institutionalisation should not be the first option [S] ([NHG](https://www.nhghealth.com.sg/News/what-happens-when-families-can-t-cope-with-caregiving)).

The recommendation is to include it as a range, never a point, labelled "subsidised beds at government-funded homes, after assessment, rates from 1 Jul 2026". Compute $4,900 × (1 − subsidy), list offsets as "may apply", bound the range with Ren Ci's published bills, show the private market range separately, place it beside home and community costs without ranking, and point to the MSW or AIC. A worked check shows why a range is needed: an SC born before 1970 with PCHI of $1,200 gets 80% off $4,900, leaving $980. Subtracting CareShield Life ($689) and MediSave Care ($200) leaves about $91, below Ren Ci's lowest published subsidised bill of $620 [calc]. The arithmetic and the real bills disagree, so a point figure would mislead.

### (c) Provider and location data: hand-curate a short list, link out for the long tail

The plan to map providers from data.gov.sg fails on inspection. The researcher pulled all **5,608 datasets** in the data.gov.sg v2 catalogue and scanned every name. "Eldercare Services" has 133 features, all edited on **2016-07-28**, with 46 "Senior Activity Centre" names, 9 "Cluster Support" entries and no AAC or AIC Link names. Its 2024-06-06 "last updated" stamp reflects a platform migration, not a data refresh. There is no SHP or KML variant, and no dataset for AACs, AIC Links, nursing homes, community hospitals, day care, hospitals or a full polyclinic list ([data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_f0fd1b3643ed8bd34bd403dedd7c1533/view)) [V]. AIC publishes AACs through its interactive E-Care locator, with no bulk download found [S] ([AIC](https://aic.sg/care-services/active-ageing-centres)). Dementia Go-To-Points includes about 82 AACs, but against more than 230 AACs that is a partial list, and "nearest AAC" built from it would send people past closer centres ([data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_e2d10e591bc8560b8946a0da24790fa5/view)) [V].

The smallest honest answer is to match the data to what "exactly who to call" means in each branch. In hospital, the answer is the ward MSW, which needs no data. For everyone, it is AIC's hotline, the five remaining AIC Links, the ten community hospitals and the acute hospitals. These are short, stable lists to curate by hand as dated JSON with a "last checked" field. Family Service Centres (55 points, edited Sep to Oct 2026) and Community Clubs (128 points, to Sep 2026) are current open layers and can supply "nearest" results directly ([FSC](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_add23c06f7267e799185c79ccaa2099b/view); [CC](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_9de02d3fb33d96da1855f4fbef549a0f/view)) [V]. For AACs, day care centres and individual nursing homes, the site should hand off to AIC's E-Care locator rather than ship a list it cannot keep current. Curating more than 150 senior care centres and 91 nursing homes by hand would be a maintenance job, not a build task. Retire the 2016 Eldercare Services layer from the design entirely. Bundle any open layers at build time, because data.gov.sg allows only **2 download calls per 10 seconds without a key** and returns HTTP 429 above that ([data.gov.sg rate limits](https://guide.data.gov.sg/developer-guide/api-overview/api-rate-limits)) [V].

### (d) The serverless postal-lookup function: drop it for launch, keep it as the fallback plan

A live call on 24 Nov 2026 to OneMap's `/api/common/elastic/search` with a postal code returned full address and coordinates without a token. It also returned the message "Authentication token missing. Please create an account and generate or renew your API Token." [V] ([OneMap API docs](https://www.onemap.gov.sg/apidocs/)). Every other useful endpoint (themes, routing, reverse geocode, population) returns HTTP 401 without a token. Tokens last three days, do not auto-renew and are issued against an account email and password that must not be exposed client-side [V]. The terms add two risks: users "must use the credentials issued to you", and access can be suspended "for any reason whatsoever" ([OneMap API ToS](https://www.onemap.gov.sg/legal/apitermsofservice.html)) [V]. Calling without a token works today, but the terms do not clearly endorse it and the warning suggests enforcement could come.

The serverless function existed to hide a token, and the launch design needs only a postal-code-to-coordinates lookup. With "who to call" reduced to short curated lists, distance precision matters little. The recommendation is to call the token-free search from the browser, treat any error or missing result as a normal case, and fall back to a region or planning-area picker so the page never breaks. The serverless proxy then becomes a documented upgrade to switch on if OneMap starts enforcing tokens. Two side notes: the postal code goes to a government server, which the privacy note should state, and the attribution footer should name OneMap.

### (e) Sessions-per-week defaults and price references: defaults are assumptions, so make them visible

No published data gives typical day-care days or home-care visits per week. The one explicit official assumption is MOH's estimate of **$945 to $1,430 a month for day care, assuming attendance every weekday** and excluding transport [S] ([ask.gov.sg](https://ask.gov.sg/moh/questions/cm2sgceyz006n46yze4zp4lia)). The Interim Caregiver Scheme runs "6 days a week and 10 to 12 hours a day" while families wait for a nursing home bed [V] ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/average-waiting-time-for-subsidised-beds-in-nursing-homes/)). AIC books home personal care in 4-hour blocks [V] ([AIC](https://www.aic.sg/care-services/home-personal-care)). With 45% of caregivers working full-time, weekday day care is the plausible default.

The recommended defaults, each shown on screen as "our assumption, change it", are day care 5 days a week (matching MOH's own basis, so the figure can be checked against it), home personal care one 4-hour block 3 times a week, home nursing once a week, and home therapy once a week for the post-stroke or post-fall branch. Convert at 4.33 weeks a month. For prices, AIC's "from" figure is the low end of every range where AIC publishes one, and the high end comes from NTUC Health's fee page or, failing that, an aggregator flagged as lower confidence. The defaults roughly agree with the sources. AIC's $55 a session × 21.7 weekdays gives about **$1,190 a month** [calc], inside both NTUC Health's $1,100 to $1,606 and MOH's $945 to $1,430. MOH's low end works out to about $43.50 a session, below AIC's current $55, so it likely predates the current price. Use AIC's figure.

### (f) Seniors per service by planning area: case-study chart only

The population side is ready. The GHS 2025 planning-area table by 5-year age band landed on data.gov.sg on 24 Nov 2026 with 388 rows [V] ([data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_c5001956eb3cba0d6805f7c28f53d4db/view)). The service side is not. With no current open layer for AACs, day care or nursing homes, the denominator would have to come from the hand-curated list or the 2016 layer, and none of the location layers carry capacity, so "seniors per centre" ignores centre size. MOH itself declined to give a seniors-to-day-care ratio when an MP asked in 2023 [V] ([MOH PQ](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/ratio-of-senior-citizens-to-available-day-care-and-community-assisted-living-services/)). As a feature, a "your area is under-served" message would be built on a guess and would alarm the user without giving them anything to do. As a case-study chart it is legitimate if it is labelled. Show 65+ and 80+ counts by planning area from GHS 2025, ranked by count rather than share. Outram and Rochor top the share ranking but are small [S] ([SingStat](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/publication-resources/population-trends-2026)). Overlay seniors per FSC or CC, which have current data, and add a seniors-per-AAC panel only after the AAC list is curated. Treat Tu Yong et al.'s spatial-mismatch study as the method reference ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.17649)) [S].

## What changes in the design, and what still needs a human check

The design documents themselves were not in the Care-navigator folder, so the changes below are stated against the design as the earlier caregiver pain-points research described it. **Audience:** widen 45 to 65 to 45 to 70, write the persona as a remote, working coordinator in their late 50s to 60s, and size type and contrast for the older end. **Flow:** add "Where is Mum right now?" as the first screen with three answers plus "already in a nursing home", fold stroke and falls into the hospital branch as reasons, and add a burnout entry that leads to respite and day care. **Provider data:** remove the data.gov.sg Eldercare Services layer, replace it with a hand-curated, dated JSON of AIC Links, community hospitals, acute hospitals and polyclinics, use the FSC and CC open layers for "nearest", and link out to AIC's E-Care locator for AACs, day care and nursing homes. Update the AIC Link list for the 30 May 2025 Maxwell and Outram closures. **Infrastructure:** drop the serverless postal function for launch, call OneMap search from the browser with a region-picker fallback, bundle all open layers at build time, and add a data-sources footer with one Open Data Licence notice per dataset plus a no-endorsement line. **Cost engine:** mark the residential $901 to $1,500 row as verified rather than inferred, seed price lows from AIC's figures ($55, $63, $25, $62, $4,900) instead of NTUC's alone, replace any $3,900 nursing home gross with $4,900, show nursing homes as a labelled range only, and display every frequency default as an editable assumption. **Case study:** add the hospital-admission sizing and MSW ratio, the planning-area chart ranked by count, and the 2026 series-break note for home care.

Several items remain unverified and must be checked by hand before launch. The [S] figures include stroke (9,702), falls (1 in 3, 2012 to 2013 data), dementia prevalence (8.8%), HCG applicants, CareShield Life's $689, ElderFund's $250, the standard MDW levy and salaries, and the 789,600 seniors total. Check the AIC Link branch list and opening status on aic.sg. Confirm that AIC's "from" prices are pre-GST or GST-inclusive, which no AIC page states, and check the aggregator prices against provider pages, especially home nursing ($62 at AIC against $80 at the aggregator). Work out how CareShield Life, MediSave Care and ElderFund stack on a nursing home bill and whether subsidy applies before payouts. Look for an official count of nursing home admissions a year, day care occupancy after 2020, and any day or home care waiting time newer than 2018. Log into OneMap with a registered account to list its themes, in case an eldercare or hospital theme exists. Read AIC's and SingStat Table Builder's terms of use. Pull Google Trends (Singapore) for "nursing home cost", "day care elderly" and "home caregiving grant", because no search-volume evidence was found. Re-check SMU ROSA's relationship split if the figures can be extracted, since it decides how many of these caregivers are adult children rather than spouses. Finally, walk through the live Care Services Recommender to confirm it still shows no personalised cost.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the project's main risk from finding the data to keeping it maintained. Population and capacity statistics are fresh and open. The things a family actually acts on (subsidy rules, prices, AIC Links, which centre to call) exist only as web pages that change at each Budget, and the one open eldercare layer has been frozen since 2016. The product's credibility therefore depends on a small set of hand-kept, dated JSON files and a re-check routine after each February Budget and March Committee of Supply. That routine should be designed in from the start, because stale figures are exactly the failure the site exists to fix.

The audience findings also tighten the pitch. The user is not a 45-year-old with time to research. It is a 60-year-old who manages the money and often has health problems of their own, who hears the news by phone, and who has days before an overstretched MSW, with about 245 elderly admissions a year, asks what the family wants to do. Read that way, the site is a briefing tool for the conversation with the MSW rather than a provider directory, and the "Where is Mum right now?" question, the nursing home range and the printable plan all follow from that.
